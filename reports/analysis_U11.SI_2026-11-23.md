## 1. Quality & Growth Filter

| Period | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | 1Q26 | 2Q26 | 3Q26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROE | 13.4% | 13.3% | 9.6% (S$2bn pre-emptive GP) | 11.5% | ~11.8% | **10.1%** (9M 11.1%) |

**Trend:** the inflection failed in 3Q26. Net profit was S$1.25bn, against consensus of ~S$1.37–1.38bn. ROE is the lowest of the three banks. (ROIC is n/m for banks, so ROE is used.)

**Catalysts, next two quarters:**
- *Fees (positive):* a record S$733m (+19% YoY), with wealth fees at S$258m. RHB notes the fastest loan growth since 2Q22 and new NPAs nearly halved QoQ.
- *NIM (the swing factor):* 1.68% in 3Q (-14bp YoY), with FY26 guidance cut to below 1.75–1.80%. The case for a NIM trough has strengthened, because the US 10-year is up ~30bp since 6 Nov, a December Fed hike is priced, and Singapore interbank rates are at 16-month highs. +10bp of NIM on ~S$540bn of interest-earning assets is ≈ +S$0.5bn pre-tax, or ≈ +8% EPS (derived).
- *FY27 guidance:* NII above FY26, loans mid-single-digit, fees mid-to-high single-digit, credit costs 25–30bp. *Negative:* opex growth at mid-to-high single digits (Citi called this a negative surprise).
- *Dividend:* the FY26 final dividend should go ex around Apr 2027, inside the window.

**Capital generation (in place of FCF yield):** CET1 is 14.6%. Leverage ratio 6.3%, LCR 152%, NSFR 112%. About 40% of the S$2bn buyback (2025–27) was done by 2Q26, and no November filings were found. At ~11% ROE and a ~50% payout, UOB retains ~5.5% of equity a year, which funds growth plus dividends with no dilution. **The run-rate dividend yield is now ~5.1%** (≈S$1.77 DPS), plus ~1% from buybacks. The UOBAM sale adds ~14bp of CET1 in 2027. Headroom over the ~14% target is ~60bp, the thinnest of the three banks.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Value-trap check.** NIM compression is **cyclical** (funding costs) and the rate tailwind is now arriving. Asset quality is the **structural watch item:**
- Group NPL is 1.5% and NPA coverage only **86%**.
- **Greater China NPL is 5.0%** (3.1% at the start of 2026), with ~40% coverage.
- Thailand: the BOT warns NPLs will stay elevated, and household debt is 86.7% of GDP. UOB economists estimate Thai 3Q GDP at +3.0%.
- New NPA formation nearly halved in 3Q (RHB), which is the first sign of stabilisation.

**Verdict: not a value trap.** Earnings are cyclically depressed and the 2025 general-allowance buffer is in place. The asset-quality tail is the reason for half-size positioning.

**Hidden assets** (none exceeds the ~S$58bn market cap; derived):
- UOBAM disposal gain of ~S$330m pre-tax, about +14bp of CET1.
- ~60bp of CET1 above the 14% target, roughly S$1.7bn (derived).
- The ex-Citi ASEAN-4 franchise (8.5m customers).
- The 2025 pre-emptive general-allowance buffer, which can be written back.
- Property revaluation surplus: data not found.

## 6. Final Verdict

**SPECULATIVE BUY (half-size, staged). Upgraded from Hold.** UOB is the weakest of the three banks on fundamentals:
- ROE is 10.1%.
- Greater China NPLs are 5.0% with only 40% coverage.
- CET1 headroom is the thinnest.
- Opex is rising in 2027.

That is why this is not a Gem. But the price now reflects much of that:
- P/E is 10.6x, below its 11.2x mean.
- P/B is 1.21x, about +0.4 SD, and the stock trades ~10% below its base justified value.
- The dividend yield is 5.1%.
- Of the three banks, UOB benefits most from the rate move now under way.

The tape shows selling exhaustion (RSI 19, volume falling on the decline) rather than the volume-expanding breakdowns at DBS and OCBC. Against a hard stop of S$33.40 below the 52-week low, base R:R is 3.1:1.

This is a counter-trend trade. There is no accumulation signature yet, so size it small and respect the stop. Add on a close above S$36.20. **Exit on a close below S$33.40.**
