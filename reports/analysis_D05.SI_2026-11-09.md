**ROE trend: dip-and-recover, now flattening.**

(FY figures from DBS results releases as cited in the August report; 9M26 from [Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-3q26-profit-dividend).) The Aug-26 inflection (17.9% in 2Q26) has **stalled**: 3Q26 net profit was S$2.81bn (-5% YoY, -9% QoQ), missing the S$2.95bn consensus, because markets trading income collapsed 65% YoY to S$152m. Quality of earnings is intact — pre-allowance profit was a record — but the "ROE re-acceleration" leg of the thesis lost a quarter. ROIC is not meaningful for a bank (deposits are raw material); ROE is the capital-efficiency proxy.

**Catalysts, next two quarters (4Q26/1Q27):**
- *NII has passed its trough:* NII +4% YoY to S$3.71bn, NIM 1.87% flat QoQ, **exit NIM 1.90%** (above the quarterly average) — first sign the margin is turning up.
- *Wealth:* AUM a record S$544bn; fees S$864m (+9% YoY but -6% QoQ — the +42% pace of 2Q26 has cooled). 18 new wealth centres roll out from 3Q26 onward.
- *Loans:* S$479bn, +11% YoY constant-currency; deposits S$650bn (+10%).
- *Trading income mean reversion:* S$152m is far below run-rate (1H26 was ~S$858m); a normalisation is a free-option upside to 4Q26 earnings.
- *FY2027 guidance:* mid-single-digit income growth, cost-income low-40s, specific provisions 17–20bp, assuming ~1.6% SORA (rising rates).

**Capital generation (FCF substitute).** CET1 15.9% transitional / 14.0% fully phased-in (16.1%/14.1% after the MAS operational-risk multiplier cut from 1.8x to 1.5x), vs a ~9% regulatory minimum — hundreds of bps of excess. Dividends S$0.81/quarter (S$0.66 ordinary + S$0.15 capital return), S$3.24 annualised, funded organically with no dilution; the S$3bn buyback continues. Note CET1 drifted down from 16.6% (2Q26) — capital return is outpacing accretion, which is by design.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Value-trap check — cyclical, not structural.** NPL ratio improved to **0.9%** (1.0% in 2Q26); NPAs S$4.63bn; specific allowances S$191m (17bp); coverage 134% (202% with collateral). The NIM decline (2.13% FY24 → 1.87%) is a rate-cycle effect now stabilising, offset by 8–11% loan growth. Asset turnover/inventory are not meaningful for a bank; the analogue — loans/deposits — is growing at ~10% with falling NPLs. No sign of structural decline.

**Hidden assets.** No land bank or cash pile exceeding market cap (market cap ≈ S$200bn). Notable optionality: 19.9% stake in Shenzhen Rural Commercial Bank (cost roughly S$1.8–2.0bn, Greater Bay Area exposure) and ~600bp+ of excess CET1. Premises revaluation surplus: data not found.

**Stress tests** (the original prompt's scenario list was truncated; standard bank scenarios used):
1. **Rate cuts resume:** guidance now assumes *rising* SORA (~1.6%). If SORA falls instead, DBS's ~S$11m/bp SGD sensitivity reopens NIM compression; fee growth must carry earnings. Impact: FY27 EPS -5–8%, fair P/B toward 2.5x → ~S$61.
2. **China/Greater Bay credit shock:** specific provisions from 17bp toward 40–60bp = ~S$1bn+ extra annual charge (≈ -9% EPS); SRCB stake impairment risk. CET1 buffer absorbs it; buyback pace would slow.
3. **Broad market sell-off / de-rating to 1.9x P/B:** ≈ S$46–48 (-33%) before earnings cuts; wealth fees and trading income would fall simultaneously. Buyback acceleration and ~4.6% yield are partial support.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD.** Fundamentals remain top-tier (NPL 0.9%, CET1 well in excess, exit NIM turning up, record wealth AUM, FY27 guidance constructive), and the Aug overbought spike has been corrected. But 3Q26 broke the earnings-acceleration narrative, the stock still trades at ~2.9x book (about 1.5–1.8x its long-run multiple), and risk/reward is 0.7–1.9 : 1 versus the 3:1 requirement. Hold for the 4.6% yield and capital return; add on dips to S$66–68 or on a volume-confirmed close above S$78.
