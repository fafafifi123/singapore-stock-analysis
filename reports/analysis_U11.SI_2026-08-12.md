# United Overseas Bank (U11.SI) — Hidden Gem Analysis

*Analysis date: 2026-08-12*

---

## 1. Quality & Growth Filter

**3-year ROE trend and inflection point**

| Period | ROE |
|---|---|
| FY2021 | 10.2% |
| FY2022 | 11.2% |
| FY2023 | 13.4% (cycle peak) |
| FY2024 | 13.3% |
| FY2025 | 9.6% (trough — S$2.0bn pre-emptive general allowances taken, mostly in 3Q25, to buffer against macro/tariff and Greater-China commercial-property uncertainty; net profit fell 23% y/y to S$4.7bn despite operating profit holding at S$7.7bn) |
| 1Q26 (annualised) | 11.5% |
| 2Q26 (annualised) | ~11.8% |

This is a textbook **inflection, not stagnation**: ROE stair-stepped up through 2021–23, air-pocketed in FY2025 on defensive (not credit-driven — NPL ratio held at 1.5% through the provisioning) balance-sheet buffering, and has been recovering sequentially through 1H26 (11.5% → ~11.8%). Management's own target is to push back toward ~14% ROE, though FY2026 guidance has been trimmed to a 12–13% range given NIM compression. [DBS Treasures](https://www.dbs.com.sg/treasures/aics/templatedata/article/equity/data/en/DBSV/012014/UOB_SP.xml), [stockbutts.com](https://stockbutts.com/u11-uob-jun-2026/), [Growbeansprout 2Q26](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend)

**Revenue catalyst for the next two quarters**

- **Wealth management fee income** is the clearest near-term engine: 1H26 wealth fees +16% y/y, with ASEAN-4 (Malaysia/Thailand/Vietnam/Indonesia — the ex-Citi footprint) growing 30%; 2Q26 net fee income hit a record S$665m (+4% q/q, +5% y/y). Management's stated goal is to **double wealth income by 2030** off a 2025 base, leveraging the ~8.5m ASEAN customers added via the Citi integration. [Growbeansprout 2Q26](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend), [The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/uob-targets-fee-led-growth-as-citi-integration-yields-8-5-million-asean-customers), [The Edge Singapore](https://www.theedgesingapore.com/capital/banking-finance/uob-gunning-double-wealth-income-2030)
- **Citi integration**: systems/platform migration across Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam and Indonesia is now largely complete (Malaysia/Thailand Nov 2022, Vietnam Mar 2023, Indonesia Nov 2023); the phase now is monetisation — cross-sell, AUM penetration, and the four markets combined are expected to add ~S$1bn of revenue on a full-year run-rate basis. [UOB Newsroom](https://www.uobgroup.com/uobgroup/newsroom/2023/uob-completes-acquisition-integration-of-citigroup-indonesia-consumer-business.page), [Thailand Business News](https://www.thailand-business-news.com/banking/306483-uob-targets-revenue-growth-as-citi-merger-adds-8-5-million-clients-across-southeast-asia)
- **NIM trajectory is a headwind, not a catalyst** for the next two quarters: NIM fell from 1.82% (1Q26) to 1.74% (2Q26), exiting July at 1.71%; full-year guidance is 1.75–1.80%, already at the lower end. The one offsetting data point is management's comment that SORA "appears to be bottoming" and could tick up in 2H26, plus aggressive deposit repricing to defend margin. [Investing.com transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-uob-posts-steady-q2-2026-results-as-revenue-beats-forecasts-93CH-4847060)
- **Loan growth** was 5% y/y in 1H26, and **fee-income growth guidance was just cut** from high-single-digit to low-single-digit for FY26 on a softer investment-banking pipeline — a genuine near-term negative that the market punished the stock for. [Growbeansprout 2Q26](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend), [Growbeansprout Aug26](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-dividend-aug-26)
- **Cost discipline**: cost-to-income ratio rose to 44.6% in FY2025 (from a 41–42% target range) mostly on margin compression rather than opex growth; management's stated ambition is to get back to "low-40s" in 2026. [The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/uob-reports-5-7-billion-net-profit-after-pre-emptive-provisions-as-fee-income-reaches-record)

**Capital generation strength (bank substitute for FCF)**

For a bank, traditional free-cash-flow analysis is not meaningful because "cash flow" is dominated by deposit-taking/lending balance-sheet mechanics rather than a capex/working-capital cycle. The correct GARP substitute is: **(a) CET1 ratio** = capital buffer available to self-fund growth without raising equity; **(b) dividend payout ratio** = capital return discipline relative to earnings; **(c) an explicit capital-return program** = evidence management itself judges the balance sheet over-capitalised relative to growth needs.

- CET1 ratio: **15.4%** as of 2Q26 (post-dividend), up from 15.3% in 1Q26 — comfortably above the ~11% regulatory floor (6.5% Basel minimum + 2.5% capital conservation buffer + 2% D-SIB surcharge that MAS applies to UOB/DBS/OCBC). This implies roughly **400+ basis points of "excess" CET1**. [Tipranks](https://www.tipranks.com/stocks/de:uob/earnings/q2-2026-report), [MAS D-SIB framework](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2015/mas-publishes-framework-for-domestic-systemically-important-banks-in-singapore)
- Dividend payout: ~50% of earnings (FY2025 total DPS S$1.56; 2Q26 interim raised to S$0.88 from S$0.85). [Edge Singapore FY25 results](https://www.theedgesingapore.com/capital/results/uob-reports-fy2025-earnings-47-bil-final-dividend-71-cents-share)
- Capital-return program: a **S$2bn buyback (2025–2027)** on top of a **S$0.50/share special dividend (S$0.8bn, paid in two 2025 tranches for UOB's 90th anniversary)** — S$3bn total, ~40% of the buyback complete (22.3m shares, S$794m) as of 2Q26. Management targets keeping CET1 "slightly above 14%" even after completing this return. A further capital top-up is coming from the **sale of UOB Asset Management to Allianz Global Investors for S$555m** (expected 2027 close, ~S$330m pre-tax gain, +~14bps CET1) — a clean example of management crystallising non-core value. [Minichart](https://www.minichart.com.sg/2025/02/20/uobs-bold-moves-special-dividend-and-2-billion-share-buyback-to-celebrate-90th-anniversary/), [Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-income-june-2026)

This is a bank that can fund its ASEAN growth ambitions, the ongoing buyback, and a ~50% payout entirely from organic capital generation — no dilution risk.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Asset quality trends**

- Group NPL ratio: **1.6%** in 2Q26, up from 1.5% in 1Q26 — modest but the direction matters.
- The real story is **Greater China**: NPL ratio there jumped to **4.8%** from 3.5% q/q, driven by the downgrade of a single closely-monitored real-estate account. UOB's Hong Kong branch has a 43% share of property-related loans, and total Greater China loan exposure was ~S$48bn as of late 2025 with NPLs already elevated (3.1% vs 2% a year earlier). UOB booked S$615m of general CRE provisions in the first nine months of 2025 and has been granting repayment deferrals/restructuring HK and mainland China real-estate loans. [Growbeansprout 2Q26](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend), [Bloomberg via Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/270103368)
- Coverage remains strong: NPA coverage 88% (306% including collateral); 2Q26 credit cost of 28bps is still inside the 25–30bps full-year guide. [Growbeansprout 2Q26](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend)

**Cyclical vs. structural**

- **NIM compression is cyclical** — a direct function of the SORA/rate-cutting cycle repricing UOB's asset book faster than its deposit book; management sees this bottoming in 2H26 and is offsetting it with deposit repricing and fee-income diversification.
- **The Greater China NPL uptick has a structural component** — it is tied to a multi-year Hong Kong/mainland China commercial-property downturn, not simply the rate cycle, and is idiosyncratic to UOB's regional CRE book rather than industry-wide Singapore credit stress. This is the more durable risk in the thesis and the main reason the market marked the stock down post-2Q26 results even as headline profit beat.

**Hidden value screen**

- **Excess capital**: ~400bps of CET1 above regulatory minimum, funding a S$3bn capital-return program with room to extend it further once the buyback completes in 2027.
- **ASEAN franchise value post-Citi**: 8.5 million ASEAN customers added, integration/platform work largely done, wealth-income-doubling target by 2030 — an embedded, multi-year fee-income call option that is not obviously priced given the stock trades close to a Gordon-Growth-model fair value (see Section 3).
- **Non-core asset monetisation**: UOB Asset Management sale to Allianz GI (S$555m, ~S$330m pre-tax gain) shows management actively surfacing value from non-lending units.
- Data not found: no granular breakdown was located for UOB's owned real-estate/property book (e.g., UOB Plaza) carrying value vs market value — cannot quantify hidden property value beyond noting it exists on the balance sheet.

- Market cap: ~S$69.3–69.6bn

**5-year historical bands**: dividend yield has ranged from ~4.6% (2021–22) to a spike of 6.35% (2023, reflecting the depressed price/high-rate period) to 5.05–5.08% (2024–25), with a 5-year average around 5.6%. **The current ~4.0–4.2% forward yield is below its 5-year average band, consistent with the stock trading near the top of its historical valuation range rather than cheap.** A precise 5-year P/E/P/B band series was **not found** in searchable form — I flag this as a genuine data gap rather than estimate it. [companiesmarketcap.com](https://companiesmarketcap.com/sgd/uob/dividend-yield/)

**DDM sanity check (conservative vs aggressive)**

Using consensus FY2026 ordinary DPS of ~S$1.72–1.76 and a ~50% payout policy:

- **Conservative case (single-stage Gordon Growth)**: fair value ≈ **S$41.95** — essentially in line with the current share price, i.e., fairly valued on a "no re-rating" basis.
- **Aggressive case (two-stage DDM, assuming faster near-term dividend growth as buyback/Citi synergies flow through before settling to a terminal growth rate)**: fair value ≈ **S$45.80**, roughly 9–11% above spot.
- **Upper-bound / outlier case (Justified P/B using a normalised sustainable ROE of ~10.6–13%)**: implies fair value as high as **S$58.85**, but this requires ROE to durably re-rate back toward the FY2023 peak (13.4%) rather than the FY2025 trough (9.6%) — a high bar given current NIM headwinds, so I treat this as an aspirational ceiling, not a base case. Source cross-checked via [stockbutts.com](https://stockbutts.com/u11-uob-jun-2026/); note that the article's own conclusion — that the market is currently pricing in a sustainable ROE of only ~7.6%, ~400bps below the 5-year average — is a useful sentiment gauge: **the market is pricing UOB more pessimistically than its own recent operating trend justifies, but not dramatically so.**

**6-month price target range**: **S$41 (downside/flat case) to S$46–47 (bull re-rating case)**, with consensus sell-side average around **S$42.5–43.5** (15 analysts; 4 Buy / 10 Hold / 1 Sell; high estimate S$50, low S$34–35). Most recent single-analyst move: **Morgan Stanley downgraded to Equalweight from Overweight in late July 2026 while raising its price target to S$46.70** (from S$43.80) — a "priced-in" call rather than a bearish one. Macquarie's target is S$45.16. ## 5. Execution & Stress Test

**Risk/reward at current price (~S$42.00)**

| Scenario | 6-month target | Stop-loss (support) | Upside | Downside | R:R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base/consensus case | S$43.50 | S$39.65 | S$1.50 | S$2.35 | **0.6:1 — does NOT clear 3:1** |
| Bull/re-rating case (MS/Macquarie targets) | S$46.70 | S$39.65 | S$4.70 | S$2.35 | **~2.0:1 — still does NOT clear 3:1** |

Honest conclusion: **at the current price, near its all-time high, the risk/reward does not clear the 3:1 bar under any of the scenarios modelled.** A 3:1 setup would only emerge on a pullback toward the S$37–38 area (closer to the conservative DDM/NAV-anchored fair value) against the same S$39.65/33.25 support structure, or if the bull-case target were extended toward the S$50 high-end analyst estimate.

**Stress tests**

1. **Rate-cut scenario (further/faster than guided)**: Directly negative for NIM — UOB already guides 1.75–1.80% for FY26, down from 1.85–1.90% in FY25, and NIM has undershot toward 1.71% exiting July. A deeper cut cycle would pressure NII further, though management's deposit-repricing playbook and growing fee-income mix (wealth +16% y/y) partially offset it. This is the base-case risk already reflected in guidance, not a tail risk.
2. **Regional credit-quality shock (Greater China CRE deterioration)**: This is the thesis's most concrete near-term risk given it just materialised in 2Q26 (Greater China NPL 3.5%→4.8%). A broader HK/China commercial property downturn would likely require additional pre-emptive general provisions similar to FY2025's S$2.0bn — which would compress ROE again (as happened in FY2025, 13.3%→9.6%) without necessarily threatening capital adequacy, given the 400bp+ CET1 cushion. Credit costs are still within the 25–30bps guide for now, so this is a monitorable risk, not yet a crisis.
3. **Broad market selloff**: UOB, as a large-cap D-SIB with an ~S$69bn market cap and high index weighting, would likely fall with the broader STI/financials complex regardless of company-specific fundamentals — beta risk rather than idiosyncratic risk. The 50% payout policy and ongoing buyback provide some valuation support (buyback continuing to retire shares into weakness), and the 400bp+ excess CET1 buffer means a selloff would not force a capital raise or dividend cut absent an actual credit event.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD** (tactical: watch for a better entry closer to S$38–39; not a "Strong Buy" gem at current levels, and not a value trap).

UOB is a fundamentally solid, well-capitalised D-SIB with a genuine multi-year growth story (Citi-driven ASEAN wealth franchise, 8.5m new customers, a credible path to doubling wealth income by 2030, and a self-funded S$3bn capital-return program backed by 400+bps of excess CET1) — the "quality and growth" and "capital generation" pillars of the GARP thesis are intact and even inflecting positively after the FY2025 provisioning-driven ROE trough. However, this is not a genuinely "hidden" opportunity: it is a heavily covered large-cap trading within ~7% of its all-time high, with a Hold-consensus sell-side rating, a dividend yield below its 5-year average band, a DDM conservative case that lands almost exactly on the current price (implying limited margin of safety), and a risk/reward profile that fails the 3:1 screen against realistic support levels. The freshly-emerged Greater China commercial real estate NPL uptick (3.5%→4.8% in a single quarter) is a live, structural — not merely cyclical — risk that the market is right to be pricing with some caution, and management's own fee-income guidance cut adds near-term earnings uncertainty. The combination of full-ish valuation, unresolved credit-quality questions in one segment, and unfavorable near-term risk/reward argues for patience over a "Strong Buy" call, while the strong capital position, growth optionality, and buyback support argue against calling it a value trap.
