# United Overseas Bank (U11.SI) — Hidden Gem Analysis

*Analysis date: 19 October 2026. Figures in S$ unless stated. Reference price: **S$41.80** (19 Oct 2026, 11:50 SGT, [Beansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/quote/U11.SI)). Latest reported results: 2Q26 (7 Aug 2026); **3Q26 results expected second week of November 2026** ([Kopi Notes](https://thekopinotes.com/articles/news/singapore-banks-october-2026-selloff/)).*

**Data caveat:** broker-code and daily foreign net-flow data are not published for SGX in any source I could reach; volume history was unavailable. Missing items are marked "not found". BVPS (~S$33.5) is back-solved from the cited P/B of 1.20x at ~S$40.25 (other sources quote 1.3–1.5x, so treat BVPS as ±8%).

---

## 1. Quality & Growth Filter

**ROE:** 2Q26 ROE ~**11.8%** (single secondary source, not corroborated). FY23–FY25 ROE series was not retrievable this run; UOB's ROE has historically sat ~1–5 points below DBS/OCBC. 2Q26 net profit S$1.48bn (+10% YoY) but 1H26 only S$2.92bn (+3%), so the improvement is recent and narrow, not yet an established inflection.

**Catalysts (next two quarters):**
- Wealth fees at a record (1H26 wealth income +16%), but **full-year fee guidance was cut** to low-single-digit growth from high-single ([Beansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend)). Less of a growth story than DBS/OCBC.
- Sale of UOB Asset Management to Allianz GI for S$555m: ~S$330m pre-tax gain, ~+14bps CET1 (completes 2027).
- Headwind: NIM 1.74% in 2Q26, exiting July at ~1.71%, at/below the bottom of the 1.75–1.80% FY guide.

**Cash strength:** CET1 15.4% post-dividend. Interim dividend S$0.88 (+3.5%), ~50% payout. FY26/27 yield estimates 4.4% / 4.6% ([Beansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-share-price-oct-2026)), no dilution. Loan growth guide is only low single-digit, so capital is accumulating faster than it is being deployed.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

- **Value-trap check:** the only one of the three with visible credit slippage. Group NPL 1.6% (from 1.5%); Greater China NPL **4.8%** (from 3.5%) on one real-estate account; credit cost 28bps (guide 25–30bps). Cyclical, but needs watching.
- **Hidden assets:** UOBAM sale crystallises hidden value (~S$330m gain, about 1% of market cap, ~S$70bn). Stakes in Great Eastern-type subsidiaries or land are not found in data this run. **No asset cushion above market cap.** The "cheapness" is purely P/B.

- Conservative (ROE 10.5%, g 3%, COE 9.5%): 1.15x → **~S$38.5**
- Aggressive (ROE 12.5%, g 3%, COE 8%): 1.9x → **~S$64**

- Range S$38–64; the aggressive end requires a ROE re-rating UOB has not delivered.

**6-month target (to Apr 2027): S$45** base (+7.7%); bull **S$49** (+17.2%, ~1.45x P/B, ROE re-rating plus UOBAM gain); bear **S$38.5** (−7.9%, back to conservative value). The ~39% consensus upside cited elsewhere appears stale.

**Sentiment:** UOB fell ~7.9% over 7–8 Oct to ~S$40.25, then rebounded ~3.9% to S$41.80, the strongest recovery of the three. It peaked in July and did not make the early-Sept highs DBS/OCBC did, so there is less profit-taking overhang. Dividend yield (~4.4%) is higher than OCBC's.

## 4. Bandarmology & Momentum

- **Accumulation/Distribution (60 days):** volume data not found. Price action: July peak → post-2Q26 dip → 8 Oct low S$40.25 → 3.9% rebound in 7 sessions. Relative strength versus OCBC (flat) and DBS (+1.3%) is a **mild positive sign of buying at the lows**, not proof of hidden accumulation.
- **Foreign flow:** not found.
- **Pivot:** **~S$43.7** (pre-sell-off level, derived from the 7.9% drop). A close above ~S$43.5 on rising volume confirms the base. Support: S$40.25 (8 Oct low), then S$38.5–39.

## 5. Execution & Stress Test

- Entry S$41.80; Hard stop **S$39.50** (below the 8 Oct low; −5.5%, S$2.30 risk).
- Reward: base S$45 → S$3.20 = **1.4:1**; bull S$49 → S$7.20 = **3.1:1**. Again passes 3:1 only on the bull path.

**Stress tests:**
1. *Rates/yields rise further:* lowest P/B of the three limits multiple downside (~1.1x → S$37), but its 5-year range is narrow, so cushion is smaller than the headline cheapness implies.
2. *SORA falls, NIM → 1.65%:* UOB has the least fee income to offset (fee guide just cut), so the NII hit would fall straight through to earnings.
3. *Greater China credit deterioration:* Greater China NPL already 4.8%; credit costs above the 30bps guide would cap the dividend growth story.
4. *Market sell-off:* wealth and loan-driven earnings are less market-sensitive than DBS; relatively defensive, but still a ~20% STI-weight bank.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD.** UOB is the cheapest of the three on P/B and yield (~4.4%), CET1 is strong (15.4%), and the UOBAM sale adds a one-off gain. But ROE (~11.8%) is clearly the lowest, fee guidance was cut, Greater China NPLs are rising, and NIM is still falling, so this is a value-with-a-reason name, not a Gem. Risk/reward is 1.4:1 base, 3.1:1 bull only. Would upgrade to Speculative Buy on a pullback to S$39.5–40.5 (near the 8 Oct low) or after a 3Q print showing NIM stabilised at ≥1.72% and a clean Greater China book. Breakout confirmation above ~S$43.5.
