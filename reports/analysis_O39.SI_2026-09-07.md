## 1. Quality & Growth Filter

**ROE trend — no new data point, still inflecting up.** OCBC's 3Q26 results are not due until **6 November 2026** ([Investing.com earnings calendar](https://www.investing.com/equities/oversea-chinese-banking-corp.-earnings)), so the ROE series is unchanged from last week: FY2023 13.27% → FY2024 13.37% → FY2025 12.20% (dip) → 1H2025 12.6% annualised → **1H2026 13.7% annualised** ([OCBC 1H26 results release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent); corroborated by [My Sweet Retirement summary](https://mysweetretirement.com/ocbc-first-half-2026-financial-results)). Two concrete near-term catalysts for 3Q/4Q26: (i) continued wealth-management/Global Markets fee momentum — OCBC just launched a dedicated **Securities Financing Unit**, the first such specialist unit among Singapore banks, aggregating lendable securities across OCBC Bank, OCBC Securities, Bank of Singapore and Great Eastern to capture institutional securities-lending fee income ([OCBC newsroom](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-forms-specialist-securities-financing-unit.page); [Citigroup press release](https://www.citigroup.com/global/news/press-release/2026/ocbc-strengthens-institutional-capabilities-new-securities-lending-program-citi-securities-lending-access-platform)) — genuinely new, though its P&L contribution over the next two quarters is likely incremental; and (ii) the pending **HSBC Indonesia retail & wealth acquisition** (S$6.6bn AUM, signed 4 May 2026, ~336,000 customers), though completion is targeted for **2Q2027**, so it is not a near-term catalyst ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-04/ocbc-to-acquire-hsbc-indonesia-s-retail-wealth-assets); [OCBC newsroom](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-acquires-hsbc-international-wealth-and-premier-banking-indonesia)). ## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Asset quality — stable, cyclical watch-item unchanged.** NPL ratio remains **0.9%**, coverage **163%** (up from 151% in 4Q25 after a S$34m Indonesia-related general-provision top-up in 2Q26) — unchanged since 31 Aug, no new quarter reported. [Citi's assessment](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/ocbc-stable-npl-eased-concerns-001319408.html) that OCBC's "stable" NPL trend has "eased some concerns" about SG banks' CRE exposure remains the operative read. The **structural risk to monitor, not yet crystallised**: Hong Kong office CRE exposure ~2.0% of OCBC's loan book — highest among the three SG banks (DBS ~0.6%, UOB ~1.3%) — with LTV <50% and at-least-annual revaluation. This week brought incremental background news reinforcing this is a live sector risk: **Road King defaulted on bond coupons** (first HK city-based developer default since the 2021 property-debt crisis began), and **New World Development** narrowly avoided default via an $11.2bn refinancing; S&P flagged further small-developer default risk over 12–24 months ([Yahoo Finance/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/analysis-hong-kong-property-sector-050249785.html); [The High Street Journal](https://thehighstreetjournal.com/hong-kong-property-developers-face-rising-defaults-as-debt-pressures-mount/)). None of this shows up in OCBC's own NPL data yet, but it is moving from "risk" to "realised" for weaker names — a contagion vector worth continued monitoring.

**Hidden balance-sheet value — Great Eastern stake, essentially flat this week.** GEH (93.7%-owned) reported 1H26 net profit of **S$849.5m, +43% YoY** — unchanged, no new GEH results until ~Feb 2027. GEH's share price has been roughly **flat-to-slightly-down** since 31 Aug: ~S$20.45–20.90 through early September (vs. S$20.58 at 31 Aug), 52-week high S$22.88, low ~S$13.52–13.72 ([Google Finance](https://www.google.com/finance/quote/G07:SGX): S$20.61 as of 7 Sept 12:00pm, prior close S$20.66).
