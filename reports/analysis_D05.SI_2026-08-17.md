## 1. Quality & Growth Filter

**ROE trend — an inflection that is still playing out, not a straight line.** DBS's three-year ROE path shows a genuine trough-and-reacceleration pattern rather than smooth compounding:

|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | S$10.3bn (+26% YoY, then a record) | 18.0% | [DBS FY2023 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_full_year_net_profit_rises_26PCT_to_record_SGD_10_3_billion_return_on_equity_rises_to_18_0pct) |
| FY2024 | S$11.4bn (+11% YoY, record) | 18.0% (sustained) | [DBS FY2024 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_full_year_net_profit_up_11pct_to_new_high_of_SGD_11_4_billion_return_on_equity_of_18_0pct_sustained_at_previous_years_record) |
| FY2025 | S$11.0bn (PBT record S$13.1bn) | **16.2%** (trough) | [DBS FY2025 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/Full_year_profit_before_tax_at_record_SGD_13_1_billion_net_profit_at_SGD_11_0_billion_with_ROE_at_16_2pct) |
| 1Q2026 | S$2.93bn (+1% YoY) | 17.0% (ROTE 18.7%) | [DBS 1Q26 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/First_quarter_net_profit_rises_to_SGD_2_93_billion_as_total_income_reaches_a_new_high_led_by_record_wealth_management_performance_ROE_at_17_0pct) |
| 2Q2026 | S$3.08bn (+9% YoY, record) | **17.9%** (ROTE 19.2%) | [DBS 2Q26 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_second_quarter_net_profit_up_9pct_to_record_SGD_3_08_billion_as_quarterly_total_income_crosses_SGD_6_billion_for_first_time) |
| 1H2026 | S$6.01bn (+5% YoY, record) | **17.5%** | [DBS 2Q26 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_second_quarter_net_profit_up_9pct_to_record_SGD_3_08_billion_as_quarterly_total_income_crosses_SGD_6_billion_for_first_time) |

Note: a bank's "invested capital" is effectively its regulatory equity capital funding risk-weighted assets — ROE (not a manufacturing-style ROIC) is the correct capital-efficiency metric here, and it is used throughout this report for that reason.

The pattern is clear: ROE peaked at a record 18.0% in FY2023–24 on the back of the global rate-hike cycle, troughed at 16.2% in FY2025 as NIM compressed with falling rates, and has since **re-accelerated for two consecutive quarters** (17.0% → 17.9%) — not because rates reversed, but because of a structural mix-shift toward fee income. This is the inflection a GARP screen should be looking for, and it remains intact five trading days after the prior edition of this report.

**Specific revenue catalyst for 3Q/4Q 2026: wealth management fee income and a management-confirmed guidance upgrade.** In 2Q26, net fee income rose **25% YoY to S$1.46bn**, with wealth management fees up **42% YoY to a record S$919m**; group wealth AUM crossed **S$516bn for the first time** (+16% YoY constant-currency), a S$500bn milestone DBS had targeted for 2027 and hit more than a year early ([DBS 2Q26 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_second_quarter_net_profit_up_9pct_to_record_SGD_3_08_billion_as_quarterly_total_income_crosses_SGD_6_billion_for_first_time); [Growbeansprout 2Q26 quick take](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-2q26-profit-dividend)). Management **raised FY2026 guidance** at the results call: total income is now projected to **exceed** 2025's level (previously guided only to match it), and commercial-book non-interest income growth guidance was lifted to the **"mid-teens"** from a prior high-single-digit range ([Growbeansprout 2Q26 quick take](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-2q26-profit-dividend)). A second, dated physical catalyst: DBS is opening **18 new wealth centres** (plus upgrading 36 existing ones) across Singapore, Hong Kong, mainland China, India, Indonesia and Taiwan — the largest physical expansion of its wealth franchise to date — with the **first wave opening from 3Q2026** ([DBS newsroom](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_to_launch_18_new_wealth_centres_across_the_region_by_2027_as_affluent_clients_seek_closer_relationships)). UOB Kay Hian independently raised its FY2026/27 profit estimates by 3.9%/3.5% post-results, citing a steadying NIM in 2H26 plus higher non-interest income as the drivers ([nextinsight.net, 11 Aug 2026](https://nextinsight.net/story-archive-mainmenu-60/949-2026/16835-analysts-say-buy-sell-hold-11-aug)).

**Capital strength: self-funding growth and payout with a large buffer over the regulatory floor.** DBS's CET1 ratio was **16.6% (transitional) / 14.6% (fully phased-in)** at 2Q26, against the **MAS regulatory minimum CET1 requirement of 9.0%** for Domestic Systemically Important Banks under MAS Notice 637 ([MAS D-SIB framework](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2015/mas-publishes-framework-for-domestic-systemically-important-banks-in-singapore)) — an excess buffer of roughly **560–760 basis points**. This funds an **S$8bn capital-return programme** (an S$3bn share buyback, plus S$5bn of S$0.15/share quarterly capital-return dividends committed through FY2027) entirely from organic capital generation, with **no dilution**: the FY2025 AGM disclosed the buyback was ~12% executed as of April 2026, with management explicitly stepping up repurchase pace during the 2025 "Liberation Day" tariff-driven dip ([Fifth Person — 2026 DBS AGM notes](https://fifthperson.com/2026-dbs-group-agm/)). Full-year FY2025 total dividends rose 38% YoY to S$3.06/share (S$2.46 ordinary + S$0.60 capital return); the 2Q26 quarterly payout held at S$0.81 (S$0.66 ordinary + S$0.15 capital return), payable 25 August 2026 ([Growbeansprout 2Q26 quick take](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-2q26-profit-dividend); [Minichart — SG banks dividends/valuations, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.minichart.com.sg/2026/08/11/singapore-bank-stocks-to-watch-as-dividends-shine-but-valuations-cap-upside/)).

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Value-trap check: asset quality is stable, not deteriorating — the pressure is cyclical, not structural.** NPL ratio has held essentially flat and low through the cycle: ~1.1% (FY2023–24) → 1.0% (FY2025, an improvement despite a prudent Q4 downgrade of a watchlisted real-estate exposure) → **1.0%** (stable for a third straight quarter through 2Q26) ([DBS FY2025 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/Full_year_profit_before_tax_at_record_SGD_13_1_billion_net_profit_at_SGD_11_0_billion_with_ROE_at_16_2pct); [DBS 2Q26 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_second_quarter_net_profit_up_9pct_to_record_SGD_3_08_billion_as_quarterly_total_income_crosses_SGD_6_billion_for_first_time)). Allowance coverage is **130% (196% including collateral)**, with specific allowances of just **16bps of loans in 2Q26** — well within historical norms ([DBS 2Q26 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_second_quarter_net_profit_up_9pct_to_record_SGD_3_08_billion_as_quarterly_total_income_crosses_SGD_6_billion_for_first_time)). Loan growth remained genuine and broad-based (loans +8% YoY constant-currency to ~S$469bn, deposits +11% YoY to S$638bn), while NIM compressed to **1.87% in 2Q26 (-18bps YoY)**, tracking falling SORA/global rates rather than any credit-quality signal ([DBS 2Q26 results](https://www.dbs.com/newsroom/DBS_second_quarter_net_profit_up_9pct_to_record_SGD_3_08_billion_as_quarterly_total_income_crosses_SGD_6_billion_for_first_time)). This is a textbook cyclical (rate-cycle) margin story being volume- and fee-offset, not a structural credit problem — corroborated by two consecutive record-profit quarters.

**Hidden assets screen:**
- **Excess regulatory capital**: ~560–760bps of CET1 above the 9.0% MAS minimum — capacity the market may not be fully crediting given the stock already trades at a premium P/B (Section 3).
- **Shenzhen Rural Commercial Bank (SRCB) stake**: grown from an initial 13% (2021, ~US$763m/RMB5.2bn) to **19.9% as of February 2026**, via incremental purchases including a further RMB310m (~S$58m/US$44.7m) tranche in Feb 2026 ([Caproasia, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.caproasia.com/2026/02/10/singapore-largest-banking-group-130-billion-dbs-buys-additional-0-5-stake-in-12-billion-shenzhen-rural-commercial-bank-for-44-7-million-cny-310-million-with-total-shareholding-increasing-to-19-9/); [Yahoo/Caproasia](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/dbs-grows-stake-shenzhen-rural-015359735.html)). A largely under-priced Greater Bay Area optionality asset that most SGX-focused retail coverage does not explicitly value.
- **Property/premises revaluation surplus**: data not found. No source disclosed a market-vs-book revaluation gap on DBS's owned premises in this research session; flagging rather than estimating.

**The P/B read is the single most important valuation fact.** DBS trades at roughly **1.6x its own 5-year average P/B and ~2.2x its 10-year median** — a materially higher multiple of book for broadly the same ROE range the stock has traded on for years, compressing the margin of safety even though the ROE re-acceleration itself is real.

**2-stage DDM sanity check** (dividend run-rate S$3.24/share annualized; cost of equity anchored to the Singapore 10-year yield of ~2.32%–2.43% ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/singapore/government-bond-yield)) plus an equity risk premium; BVPS ≈S$25 derived from FY2025 net profit S$11.0bn ÷ 16.2% ROE ÷ ~2.83bn shares):

- **Conservative** (COE 9.0%, dividend flat ~S$3.10–3.30 through 2027 then tapering toward ~S$2.90 post-FY2027, terminal growth 2.5%): fair value ≈ **S$43–45**.
- **Aggressive** (COE 7.5%, ordinary dividend growth ~5–6%/yr offsetting the capital-return taper, terminal growth 3.5%): fair value ≈ **S$80–83**.
- **Justified P/B cross-check** [(ROE−g)/(COE−g)×BVPS]: conservative (ROE 15%, g 3%, COE 9%) → ~2.0x×S$25 ≈ **S$50**; aggressive (ROE 18%, g 4%, COE 7.5%) → ~4.0x×S$25 ≈ **S$100**.

Combined range ≈**S$43–100**, with S$75.85 in the upper-middle — the market is already pricing close to the aggressive case.

## 5. Execution & Stress Test

- Entry: **S$75.85**
- Base target: ~S$77 → upside **1.5%**
- Bull target: **S$85.86** → upside **13.2%**
- Hard stop (50-day SMA ≈S$69.22) → downside **8.7%**
- Tighter stop (S$71.90 support) → downside **5.2%**

Best-case ratio (bull vs. tighter stop): **≈2.5:1**. Disciplined ratio (bull vs. 50-day SMA stop): **≈1.5:1**. Base case vs. either stop: well under 1:1. **Does not clear 3:1 at today's price**; becomes attractive on a pullback into the S$69–72 zone.

**Stress tests:**
1. **Renewed/faster rate cuts**: guidance assumes SORA ~1% with modest further easing ([UOB outlook](https://dollarbackmortgage.com/blog/how-low-sora-outlook/)); faster cuts would reaccelerate NIM compression past -18bps YoY, testable only if fee income keeps outrunning the NII hit.
2. **Regional/China credit shock**: precise China property-loan exposure — data not found; main identifiable channel is the 19.9% SRCB stake (~S$1.8–2.0bn invested), which would show up as associate-income drag before group-level allowance stress; ~560–760bp CET1 buffer absorbs this without a capital raise but would slow buybacks/earnings growth.
3. **Broad market selloff**: DBS is the largest STI constituent and a levered proxy for SG risk appetite; P/B could mean-revert toward ~1.87x (implying ~S$47–50 on unchanged book) while wealth AUM/fee income — the current growth engine — decelerates simultaneously; partial offset from accelerated buybacks (as in the 2025 "Liberation Day" episode) and the ~4.3% forward yield.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD.** DBS remains a genuine quality compounder with improving fundamentals: ROE inflected from a FY2025 trough of 16.2% to 17.5–17.9% in 1H26 on a durable, quantified catalyst (wealth AUM past S$500bn a year early, wealth fees +42% YoY to a record S$919m, raised FY2026 guidance, new wealth-centre rollout from 3Q26); asset quality is stable (NPL 1.0% for three straight quarters); and the bank self-funds an S$8bn capital-return programme from a CET1 buffer 560–760bps above the MAS minimum with zero dilution. Technicals have healthily worked off their prior overbought condition (RSI 73.5→53–67) without breaking the uptrend. But none of this makes the stock a bargain: it trades within ~3% of its all-time high, at ~3.0x book vs. a 5-year average of ~1.87x, with the 16-analyst consensus (~S$75.38) sitting essentially at spot — the fee-income-offset thesis is already substantially priced in. Even the most optimistic analyst target against the tightest defensible stop yields only ≈2.5:1, falling to ≈1.5:1 against the more conservative 50-day-SMA stop — short of the 3:1 bar either way, unchanged from the 12 August 2026 edition's conclusion. Hold for existing owners for yield and capital-return optionality; do not chase at current levels — the setup improves specifically on a pullback into the S$69–72 support zone.
