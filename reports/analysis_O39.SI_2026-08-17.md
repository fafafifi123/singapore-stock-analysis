**The inflection is real, not a base-effect illusion.** FY2025 was the stagnation/dip year — net profit *fell* 2% as NIM compressed 29bp to 1.91% (from 2.20% in FY2024) faster than deposit costs could reprice ([investingiguana](https://investingiguana.com/p/ocbc-fy2025-deep-dive-why-net-profit?action=share)). But the pace of NIM compression is now visibly decelerating and non-interest income has stepped up to compensate: NIM fell only 6bp QoQ in 2Q26 to 1.70% (vs -29bp for all of FY2025), while 2Q26 non-interest income surged 51% YoY to a record S$1.91bn, driven by trading income (+85% YoY to S$695m), wealth fees (+44% YoY to S$470m) and insurance income (+68% YoY to S$382m) ([Investing.com Q2 2026 earnings call transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-ocbc-posts-record-q2-2026-profit-as-shares-near-high-93CH-4845972); [thesingaporeaninvestor.sg 2Q/1H26 review](https://www.thesingaporeaninvestor.sg/2026/08/07/oversea-chinese-banking-corporation-limited-sgx-o39-2q-1h-fy2026-results-review/)). ROE of 14.4% in 2Q26 is now *above* the FY2023/FY2024 run-rate, not just a recovery back to it.

*Note on ROIC:* classic ROIC/FCF metrics are not meaningful analytical tools for a leveraged deposit-funded bank — leverage is the business model, not a cost of capital to be "returned." One third-party estimate puts OCBC's trailing ROIC at ~11% (dated mid-2025, likely stale) ([Finimize](https://finimize.com/content/ovchf-asset-snapshot)); I substitute ROE and the CET1/payout-capacity analysis below as the bank-appropriate quality proxies, consistent with sell-side convention.

### Revenue catalyst for 3Q/4Q 2026

1. **Wealth management / Bank of Singapore** — Banking Wealth AUM record S$343bn (FY2025, +15%), with net-new-money inflows of S$27bn in FY2025 and a further S$11bn in 1H2026 (S$6bn in 2Q26 alone). Wealth management income was S$3.29bn in 1H2026 (+27% YoY), now **41% of group income** ([Investing.com Q2 slides coverage](https://ng.investing.com/news/company-news/ocbc-q2-2026-slides-record-profit-driven-by-wealth-management-surge-93CH-2647345)). Bank of Singapore separately manages ~US$145bn AUM, having already exceeded its 2023-set target, with UHNW client AUM +25% and financial-intermediary AUM +30% recently ([Hubbis](https://www.hubbis.com/news/bank-of-singapore-targets-asia-s-top-five-private-banks-with-usd145-billion-aum); [finews.asia](https://www.finews.asia/finance/44173-bank-of-singapore-private-banking-ocbc-uhnw-eam-aum-jason-moo)).
2. **Insurance (Great Eastern)** — 2Q26 insurance income +68% YoY to S$382m on GEH new-business-embedded-value (NBEV) margin expansion; GEH FY2025 insurance income was up 17% to S$1.07bn and contributed ~S$1.2bn of group profit ([fifthperson AGM notes](https://fifthperson.com/2026-ocbc-agm/)).
3. **NIM stabilisation** — management guidance and the sequential deceleration in NIM compression (-6bp QoQ in 2Q26 vs -29bp for all FY2025) both point to a bottoming NIM in 2H2026, which would remove the single biggest earnings headwind of the past two years.
4. **Loan growth** — customer loans grew 11% YoY (5% QoQ) to S$364bn in 1H2026; management has **raised FY2026 guidance to "high-single to low-double-digit" loan growth** and tightened cost-to-income guidance to the low-40s% ([growbeansprout 2Q26 quick take](https://growbeansprout.com/ocbc-2q26-profit-dividend)).
5. **Cost discipline / credit costs** — FY2025 credit costs fell to 17bp (from 19bp in FY2024); FY2026 credit-cost guidance is 20-25bp, with management indicating the lower end is achievable ([growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/ocbc-2q26-profit-dividend)).

### Capital strength — self-funded growth and payout, no dilution

- **Fully phased-in CET1**: 14.0% at 2Q2026 (transitional basis 15.7%), down from 15.1%/16.9% at FY2025-end, reflecting the payment of FY2025 final + special dividends and RWA growth against profit accretion. Management's own target operating level is 14% fully phased-in — OCBC is running *at* its stated target, not below it (OCBC 2Q2026 results commentary, cross-referenced via search).
- MAS requires Singapore's D-SIBs (DBS, OCBC, UOB) to hold a minimum CET1 of 6.5% ([MAS 2015 D-SIB framework release](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2015/mas-publishes-framework-for-domestic-systemically-important-banks-in-singapore)), plus a 2.5% capital conservation buffer — an effective ~9.0% floor before discretionary-distribution restrictions bite, the reference point cited in this report's brief. OCBC's 14.0% fully-phased CET1 therefore carries a **~5.0 percentage-point buffer** (≈S$7-8bn of capital in RWA terms) above that floor.
- **Capital return program**: a dedicated S$2.5bn capital-return plan (special dividends + buybacks, 2024-2026) has ~S$800m left, which management has committed to complete by FY2026 either via buybacks or an estimated ~18-cent/share special dividend if unused ([The Edge Singapore](https://www.theedgesingapore.com/capital/banking-finance/ocbc-open-returning-share-buyback-portion-800-mil-special-dividends-hsbc)). FY2025 total payout ratio was already 60% (50% ordinary + 10% special) — S$0.99/share total dividend, up from S$1.01 in FY2024 essentially flat — with 1H2026 interim dividend raised 15% YoY to 47 cents on a 50% ordinary payout ratio.
- **Conclusion**: OCBC is self-funding double-digit loan growth *and* a multi-year capital-return program purely from retained earnings, with CET1 sitting comfortably above both the regulatory floor and management's own target — no equity-dilution risk is evident in any base case.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

### Value-trap check: cyclical, not structural

- **NPL ratio**: 1.0% (FY2023) → 0.9% (FY2024) → 0.9% (FY2025) → 0.9% (1Q26) → **0.9%** (2Q26) — stable for at least the last 6 consecutive quarters, and the lowest among the three Singapore banks ([thesingaporeaninvestor.sg](https://www.thesingaporeaninvestor.sg/2026/08/07/oversea-chinese-banking-corporation-limited-sgx-o39-2q-1h-fy2026-results-review/)).
- **Allowance/NPA coverage**: 159% (FY2024) → 151% (FY2025) → **163%** (2Q26) — coverage has actually *strengthened* even as the NPL ratio stayed flat, a genuinely reassuring signal for a value-trap check ([OCBC FY2025 release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2025/ocbc-group-full-year-2025.page); [growbeansprout 2Q26 quick take](https://growbeansprout.com/ocbc-2q26-profit-dividend)).
- **NIM vs loan growth**: the share-price weakness that characterized 2025 (NIM -29bp to 1.91%, net profit -2%) tracked the SORA/global rate-cutting cycle, not credit quality — NPLs did not move at all through the compression. This is a textbook **cyclical**, not structural, drag, and it is already decelerating (2Q26 NIM down only 6bp QoQ vs 22bp YoY). Loan growth has also *accelerated* through the NIM downturn (8% FY2024 → 9% FY2025 → 11% YoY in 1H2026), the opposite of what a genuine credit-quality problem would produce.
- **Independent bear counterpoint** (for balance): one analyst flagged that FY2025's 60% payout ratio is already at the top of management's stated range, and that "if global rates drop another 100bp," NIM could fall toward 1.80% and pressure dividend sustainability ([investingiguana FY2025 deep-dive](https://investingiguana.com/p/ocbc-fy2025-deep-dive-why-net-profit?action=share)) — a fair risk to flag even though 2Q26 data (NIM 1.70%, still above that stress level, and non-II offsetting) has so far outrun this concern.

### Hidden assets screen

- **Excess regulatory capital**: as above, ~5.0pp of fully-phased CET1 buffer above the ~9% regulatory floor (~S$7-8bn) is real, verifiable excess capital funding the buyback/special-dividend program.
- **Great Eastern Holdings (GEH) — the "special situation" has been resolved, not advanced.** Timeline: May 2024 voluntary offer at S$25.60/share → GEH free float fell below 10%, trading suspended July 2024 → June 2025 "final" conditional delisting exit offer at S$30.15/share (S$0.9bn) for the remaining 6.28% ([OCBC media release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2025/ocbc-backs-great-eastern-delisting-proposal); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-06/great-eastern-plans-delisting-with-700-million-ocbc-backed-bid)) → **delisting vote failed** in July 2025 (63.5% minority approval vs. 75% required) ([Fintech Singapore](https://fintechnews.sg/113649/insurtech/ocbc-great-eastern/)) → GEH restored its free float via a 1-for-1 bonus issue (ordinary shares or Class C non-voting shares), completed by 25 August 2025, with trading resuming 21 August 2025 ([The Edge Singapore](https://www.theedgesingapore.com/news/banking-finance/great-eastern-holdings-resume-trading-9am-august-21-subject-sgx-approval)). **Current stake**: OCBC retains 93.72% of GEH's economic interest/dividend rights, but its *voting* share fell to 88.19% after electing Class C non-voting shares for part of its holding (search-verified detail, mechanics not independently re-confirmed via primary filing due to a blocked fetch). New Group CEO Tan Teck Long has explicitly called GEH "a chapter behind" OCBC, confirming **no further privatisation bid is planned** ([Yahoo Finance/Edge Singapore](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/great-eastern-chapter-behind-ocbc-201336496.html)). **Implication for this report**: the GEH stake is fully consolidated on OCBC's balance sheet (not an off-balance-sheet minority holding), so there is no unlocked "sum-of-the-parts" arbitrage left to capture — the once-live M&A optionality is now closed, and GEH should be modelled simply as a steadily growing, already-consolidated insurance income stream (+68% YoY in 2Q26), not as a forward catalyst.
- **Bank of Singapore private-banking franchise**: ~US$145bn AUM, already past its own 2023-set medium-term target, growing >20% recently with UHNW +25%/FIM +30% ([Hubbis](https://www.hubbis.com/news/bank-of-singapore-targets-asia-s-top-five-private-banks-with-usd145-billion-aum); [finews.asia](https://www.finews.asia/finance/44173-bank-of-singapore-private-banking-ocbc-uhnw-eam-aum-jason-moo)) — this is a genuine, still-compounding franchise asset, though it is fully embedded in group wealth-income figures already reflected in the share price re-rating (see Section 3), so it is not "hidden" in the sense of being unpriced.
- Data not found: no standalone sell-side sum-of-the-parts valuation isolating Bank of Singapore or GEH against OCBC's core banking business was located in this search session.

### 2-stage DDM sanity check

Base dividend D₀ = S$1.00/share (approximating the FY2026E run-rate: 47¢ interim + an estimated ~53¢ final/special, consistent with consensus FY2026 DPS estimate of ~S$0.978 and management's 50% ordinary payout guidance).

- **Conservative case**: 2% dividend growth for 5 years, 2.0% terminal growth, 9.5% cost of equity → **fair value ≈ S$13.60/share**
- **Aggressive case**: 7% dividend growth for 5 years, 3.5% terminal growth, 8.0% cost of equity → **fair value ≈ S$26.80/share**
- Current price (S$31.24) sits **above even the aggressive DDM case** — reinforcing the "priced for perfection" read.

**Justified-P/B cross-check** (Gordon: justified P/B = (ROE − g) / (r − g)):
- Using the FY2025-normalized run-rate (ROE 13%, g 3%, r 9%): justified P/B ≈ (0.13−0.03)/(0.09−0.03) = **1.67x** → implied fair value ≈ S$23.70 (on BVPS S$14.20).
- Using the optimistic 2Q26 run-rate (ROE 14.4%, g 4%, r 8.5%): justified P/B ≈ (0.144−0.04)/(0.085−0.04) = **2.31x** → implied fair value ≈ S$32.80 — only under this most-optimistic, single-best-quarter-annualized assumption set does the justified-P/B framework support the current spot price. Cost-of-equity assumptions of ~9% (13% ROE / 3-4% growth) are consistent with public broker Gordon Growth Model parameters found for OCBC (search-derived; specific broker not independently confirmed).

## 5. Execution & Stress Test

### Risk/reward calculation

- Entry (today's spot): **S$31.24**
- Hard stop-loss (major technical support): **S$27.48**
- 6-month base-case target: **S$32.50** (midpoint of S$31-33 base range)
- 6-month bull-case target: **S$36.50** (midpoint of S$36-37 bull range)

| Case | Upside | Downside (to stop) | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | S$1.26 (4.0%) | S$3.76 (12.0%) | **0.34 : 1** |
| Bull | S$5.26 (16.8%) | S$3.76 (12.0%) | **1.40 : 1** |

**Neither case clears the 3:1 risk/reward threshold** — in fact the base case is skewed meaningfully against the buyer at current levels. Even pairing the bull-case target (S$36.50) against the deeper SMA50 stop (S$26.40, downside S$4.84/15.5%) only produces a ratio of ~1.09:1.

### Scenario stress tests

1. **Renewed/accelerated rate cuts**: NIM compression has been decelerating (-6bp QoQ in 2Q26 vs -29bp for FY2025), but the independent bear case notes that a further ~100bp of global rate cuts could push NIM back toward ~1.80% and pressure the dividend ([investingiguana](https://investingiguana.com/p/ocbc-fy2025-deep-dive-why-net-profit?action=share)). Given non-interest income is now 41% of group income and less rate-sensitive, the earnings hit would likely be partially cushioned versus 2025's experience, but NII (still the majority of revenue) would compress further, likely capping ROE re-acceleration around the 12-13% band rather than sustaining 14%+.
2. **Great Eastern situation resolving unfavourably**: the privatisation catalyst is closed (no further bid planned), so the primary risk here is not a failed deal but a deterioration in GEH's own underwriting/investment performance (e.g., an equity-market drawdown hitting insurance mark-to-market income, which was already noted as a headwind in early-2026 broker commentary). A meaningful GEH earnings miss would directly hit the ~68%-YoY-growing insurance income line that has been a key 2Q26 growth driver, though GEH is a small enough share of total group profit (~S$1.2bn of ~S$7.4bn FY2025) that this alone would not be thesis-breaking.
3. **Regional credit shock**: NPL (0.9%) and coverage (163%) are both currently strong, but from a very low base after two years of benign credit conditions — a regional (e.g., China/Hong Kong commercial real estate) shock could see NPL formation rise quickly. High coverage provides an earnings/capital buffer, but a shock would very likely also trigger P/B multiple compression on top of any credit-cost increase, given how far current multiples already sit above historical norms.
4. **Broad market selloff / multiple mean-reversion**: this is arguably the largest quantifiable risk. P/B mean-reverting from ~2.2-2.4x toward its own 5-year average of ~1.16x — even with zero change to underlying fundamentals — implies roughly **45-50% downside** on multiple compression alone. Given OCBC's multiples sit at roughly double their historical averages, this name currently carries materially more de-rating risk than one still trading within its historical valuation band.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD.**

OCBC's operating business genuinely qualifies as a re-accelerating quality compounder: ROE has moved from a FY2025 stagnation trough of 12.6% to 14.4% in 2Q2026 (the first quarter ever above S$2bn net profit), NIM compression is visibly decelerating, asset quality remains pristine and improving (0.9% NPL, coverage strengthened to 163%), wealth management and Bank of Singapore are compounding double-digit AUM growth, and the bank is running a ~5-percentage-point CET1 buffer above its regulatory floor that fully self-funds both double-digit loan growth and a S$2.5bn capital-return program without dilution. This is real, evidenced quality — not a story. But it is precisely the reason this is not a "hidden gem" entry point today: the market has already found this story and re-rated the stock nearly to a double off its 52-week low, to a P/B roughly double its own 5- and 10-year historical average, to a level above even the aggressive-case DDM fair value (S$26.80) and above the justified-P/B fair value under all but the single most optimistic (peak-quarter-annualized) assumption set. The Great Eastern privatisation catalyst — the one clear "special situation" optionality this name once offered — has been explicitly closed by the new CEO ("a chapter behind us"), removing rather than adding to the hidden-value case. Freshly-raised post-earnings broker targets (UOB Kay Hian S$31.60) sit essentially at today's spot price rather than meaningfully above it, RSI remains in overbought territory (72.6 as of the last confirmed reading) with a slight negative MACD divergence, and the computed risk/reward against the nearest hard technical support (S$27.48) is 0.34:1 in the base case and only 1.40:1 in the bull case — nowhere close to the 3:1 bar this framework requires. This is not a value trap (credit quality is genuinely improving, not deteriorating) and not a speculative buy (there is no open, mispriced catalyst left to trigger a re-rating) — it is a quality bank whose story is now fully in the price. The appropriate action is to continue holding/monitoring, not to add at current levels; a pullback toward the S$26-28 zone (the SMA50/prior-resistance-turned-support band) would meaningfully improve the risk/reward calculus for a fresh entry.
