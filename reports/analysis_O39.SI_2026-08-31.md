## 1. Quality & Growth Filter

**ROE trend — the 2026 inflection continues to build**

(*[OCBC 1H2026 results press release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent); [OCBC FY2025 release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2025/ocbc-group-full-year-2025.page)*)

1H FY2026 net profit hit a record **S$4.19bn (+13% YoY)**, with 2Q26 net profit of **S$2.22bn (+22% YoY, +12% QoQ)** — the first quarter ever above S$2bn ([The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/press-releases/ocbc-group-posts-13-rise-in-first-half-2026-net-profit-to-3-27-billion); [Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/ocbc-2q26-profit-dividend)). This is not a low-base effect: 2Q26 total income hit a record S$4.17bn, with non-interest income of S$1.91bn (+51% YoY) now **~46% of total income** ([Growbeansprout Q2 quick take](https://growbeansprout.com/ocbc-2q26-profit-dividend)) — a genuine mix-shift toward fee-based, less rate-sensitive revenue.

**Revenue catalysts for the next 2 quarters**
- **NIM trajectory**: 2Q26 NIM was 1.70% (‑22bps YoY, ‑6bps QoQ — a *milder* sequential decline than 1Q26's 10bps drop). H1FY26 NIM 1.73% (‑25bps YoY). Exit-June NIM was 1.67%. Management continues to guide NIM to **stabilize in H2 2026** if SORA gradually strengthens toward year-end ([search synthesis of OCBC 1H26 commentary](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-dividend-aug-26); [OCBC 1H2026 release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent)). The sequential deceleration in NIM compression is itself an early positive signal that the stabilization call is on track.
- **Loan growth**: raised again — 11% YoY on a constant-currency basis to **S$364bn** at H1FY26 (up from S$347bn at 1Q26), with full-year guidance now "high-single to low-double-digit" ([OCBC 1H2026 release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent)).
- **Wealth management**: remains the dominant growth engine (Bank of Singapore AUM record, fee income up sharply); non-interest income at 46% of total group income in 2Q26 vs 41% in the prior report's H1 figure — the mix-shift is accelerating, not plateauing.
- **Insurance (Great Eastern)**: GEH's **1H2026 profit jumped 43% YoY to S$849.5m**, "supported by stronger insurance results and improved investment performance," though the article notes headline growth was not driven entirely by the underlying insurance business — some of the gain reflects market/investment-related items ([Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/ocbc-ge-earnings-aug-2026)). GEH also raised its interim dividend and announced a restructuring of its Indonesia business to integrate more closely with the OCBC Group.
- **Cost-to-income**: improved further to **38.5%** in H1FY26 (from 38.9% a year ago) ([OCBC 1H2026 release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent)).

**Capital generation / self-funding capacity**
- Group CET1 CAR as at 30 June 2026 was **15.7% on a transitional basis** and **14.0% on a fully phased-in basis** — both down YoY from 17.0%/15.3% at 1H2025, reflecting active capital return (buybacks + special dividends), not deterioration ([OCBC 1H2026 press release, via WebFetch extraction](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent)).
- MAS requires Singapore's D-SIBs (DBS/OCBC/UOB) to hold a minimum CET1 CAR of **9.0%** (6.5% Basel minimum + 2.5% capital conservation buffer, per MAS's D-SIB framework) ([MAS D-SIB framework release](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2015/mas-publishes-framework-for-domestic-systemically-important-banks-in-singapore)). OCBC's transitional CET1 of 15.7% therefore sits **~670bps above the regulatory floor**, and even the fully phased-in 14.0% steady-state target carries a **~500bps buffer** — several billion SGD of unencumbered capital.
- **Capital return program**: the S$2.5bn (2024–2026) program remains "on track to complete... by FY26" ([OCBC 1H2026 release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent)). OCBC repurchased **21.6m shares for ~S$247m in July 2026 alone**, ranking it among Singapore's most aggressive blue-chip buyback programs in 2026 ([The Smart Investor / Yahoo Finance](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/top-3-blue-chip-sgx-033000067.html)). Roughly **S$800m remains outstanding** on the program, to be delivered via further buybacks and/or an estimated **~S$0.18/share special dividend at year-end** ([The Edge Singapore](https://www.theedgesingapore.com/capital/banking-finance/ocbc-open-returning-share-buyback-portion-800-mil-special-dividends-hsbc); [Growbeansprout 2H outlook](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-dividend-aug-26)). Interim FY2026 ordinary dividend was raised to **47 cents (+15% YoY)**, a 50% payout ratio ([OCBC 1H2026 release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent)).

*Note on ROIC/FCF*: as in the prior edition, traditional ROIC/FCF metrics are not meaningful for a leveraged deposit-taking institution; ROE/ROA plus CET1-buffer/payout-capacity remain the bank-appropriate GARP substitutes.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Asset quality — still pristine, still stable**
- NPL ratio: **0.9%**, stable for multiple consecutive quarters through H1FY26.
- NPA allowance coverage: **163%**, unchanged from the prior two quarters ([OCBC 1H2026 release](https://www.ocbc.com/group/media/release/2026/ocbc-group-first-half-2026-net-profit-grew-13percent)).
- Regional CRE watch item persists: general industry commentary continues to flag Hong Kong/Greater China commercial real estate as a "major" credit risk for regional lenders broadly, though with the caveat that Singapore banks' CRE books carry low average LTVs and are concentrated with top-tier developers ([itiger/Moody's-referencing search synthesis](https://www.itiger.com/news/2513342012)) — this is general sector commentary, not an OCBC-specific update since 12 Aug, so treat as background risk rather than a new datapoint. UOB, by contrast, explicitly flagged Greater China CRE exposure "requiring close monitoring" alongside a fee-income guidance cut in its own 2Q26 update — a useful read-across that OCBC's peer-relative asset quality currently looks comparatively better-controlled ([Growbeansprout 2H outlook comparison](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-dividend-aug-26)).

**Cyclical vs. structural**: unchanged conclusion from the prior edition — NIM pressure is cyclical (rate-cycle driven), not structural; the milder QoQ NIM decline in 2Q26 (‑6bps vs ‑10bps in 1Q26) is early evidence the trough is forming, consistent with management's H2 stabilization guidance.

**Great Eastern stake — sum-of-the-parts, and a notable 3-week re-rating**
- OCBC owns **93.7%** of Great Eastern Holdings (SGX: G07).
- GEH's 1H2026 profit surge (+43% YoY) drove a sharp re-rating: GEH shares rose from **~S$17.08 (12 Aug)** to **S$20.58 (31 Aug)** — roughly **+20% in under three weeks** ([stockanalysis.com G07 page](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sgx/G07/)). GEH market cap is now **~S$19.36bn**; OCBC's 93.7% stake is therefore worth **~S$18.1bn at market value**, up from ~S$15.2bn just three weeks earlier — this is a meaningful, verifiable appreciation in embedded asset value, not a "hidden" catalyst still to be unlocked.
- The GEH stake now represents **~13% of OCBC's S$139.5bn market cap**.
- **Privatisation optionality is now explicitly closed, not merely "shelved"**: OCBC's new CEO stated in August 2026 that Great Eastern is "a chapter behind" OCBC, and that the 93.7% stake is "good enough" for group-level collaboration, with no intention to pursue full ownership "in the foreseeable future" ([Yahoo Finance/The Edge Singapore](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/great-eastern-chapter-behind-ocbc-201336496.html)). This confirms and hardens the 12 Aug edition's read that the special-situation catalyst is off the table — but the underlying insurance subsidiary is now compounding value faster than the market previously priced, which is itself a legitimate (if already-surfaced) positive.
- Excess capital (Section 1): ~500–670bps of CET1 buffer above the 9.0% MAS D-SIB floor remains a second "hidden asset" layer, funding buybacks/special dividends and loan growth with zero dilution.

## 5. Execution & Stress Test

**Risk/reward ratio**: Using the base-case 6-month target midpoint (~S$32.5) vs. spot (~S$31.44) and a hard stop at the S$28.63 near-term support (20-day range floor):
- Upside: S$32.5 − S$31.44 = **S$1.06** (~3.4%)
- Downside to stop: S$31.44 − S$28.63 = **S$2.81** (~8.9%)
- Risk/reward ≈ **0.38 : 1 — fails the 3:1 threshold decisively**, and is in fact *worse* than the 12 Aug edition's already-failing ~0.49:1, because the price has round-tripped sideways near the highs while the nearby support has drifted up modestly (limiting downside cushion) without a matching increase in near-term upside room to the now-tested S$31.8 resistance.
- Even pairing the bull case (S$37.5) against the deeper structural support (~S$27.5): upside S$6.06 (19.3%) vs downside S$3.94 (12.5%) → ratio ≈ **1.5:1** — still short of 3:1.
- **Conclusion: no configuration of realistic 6-month target vs. realistic stop clears 3:1 at the current entry price.**

**Scenario stress tests:**

1. **Rate-cut / NIM scenario**: The bull case for H2 rests on NIM *stabilizing*, not necessarily rising — 2Q26 NIM compression already decelerated (‑6bps QoQ vs ‑10bps in 1Q26), a mild positive sign. But a faster/deeper SORA decline than currently priced would push NIM back below the 1.67% June exit print, hitting net interest income directly. Non-interest income (now ~46% of group revenue) provides a larger cushion than a year ago, but is itself partly market/investment-sensitive (as flagged in the GEH 1H26 result, where "headline growth was not driven entirely by the underlying insurance business") — so a rates-and-markets double-dip (lower NIM *and* softer investment income) is a plausible combined downside scenario not fully hedged by the income mix-shift.

2. **Regional credit shock (Greater China / HK CRE)**: NPL (0.9%) and coverage (163%) remain very strong, and OCBC's own trend has not deteriorated. But peer UOB has already flagged Greater China CRE exposure as requiring "close monitoring" alongside a Q2 fee-income guidance cut — a sector-wide risk that a market selloff could re-price across all three Singapore banks (including OCBC) even without OCBC-specific NPL deterioration, given elevated starting multiples across the group.

3. **Broad multiple-compression / market selloff**: Unchanged as the largest single risk. P/B (~2.21x) sits far above the 5-year mean (~1.13x); a de-rating back toward historical bands in a broad risk-off event would be a **~45–50% purely multiple-driven downside**, independent of any fundamental deterioration — and Singapore's rising 10-year SGS bond yield (2.30% cut-off at the 27 Aug auction, up from 2.09% in April — [Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/10-year-sgs-bond-allotment-27-aug-2026)) works against high-multiple, low-yield-relative bank equities on a relative-value basis, a headwind not present in the same form three weeks ago.

4. **Great Eastern-specific**: with privatisation optionality now explicitly closed (Section 2), GEH is no longer a special-situation catalyst for OCBC — it is simply a fast-growing, already-consolidated subsidiary. A reversal of GEH's 1H26 profit surge (management itself flagged non-recurring/market-related contribution to the 43% jump) would remove a recent tailwind to OCBC's own non-interest income line without a corresponding "deal" catalyst to fall back on.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD** (quality and growth trajectory both continue to strengthen, but the stock remains priced for a scenario that has already substantially played out).

Three weeks on from the 12 Aug edition, OCBC's operating story has gotten *better*, not worse: ROE is at 13.7% and rising, loan growth guidance keeps getting raised (now S$364bn, +11% YoY), NIM compression is visibly decelerating ahead of a guided H2 stabilization, cost-to-income keeps improving (38.5%), asset quality remains pristine (0.9% NPL / 163% coverage), the S$2.5bn capital return program remains on track with ~S$800m still to be delivered, and Great Eastern just posted a 43% profit jump that lifted the value of OCBC's 93.7% stake by roughly S$3bn in under three weeks. None of that supports a bearish fundamental view. But the valuation and technical picture that made this a "not a hidden-gem entry point" call on 12 Aug remains essentially intact: P/E (17.84x) and P/B (~2.21x) are still far above 5-year historical bands, the DDM aggressive case (~S$30) is still below spot, the stock is still consolidating just under its all-time high (S$31.79–31.86) on below-average volume with three consecutive "momentum fading" technical reads, the GEH privatisation catalyst has now been explicitly and permanently closed by new management commentary rather than merely "shelved," and — most decisively for the execution framework here — **no realistic 6-month target/stop pairing clears even 1.5:1, let alone the 3:1 threshold**, with the base-case setup actually landing worse (~0.38:1) than three weeks ago. This remains a name to admire operationally and revisit on a genuine pullback toward the S$27–29 support band, not to chase within ~1% of its own all-time high.
