Management's FY26 ROE range of 12–13% (August) now looks out of reach. Honest read: no inflection — a failed one. (ROIC n/m for banks; ROE used.)

**Catalysts, next two quarters:**
- *Fees:* record net fee income S$733m (+19% YoY, +10% QoQ); wealth income 9M +13%, credit cards +25%, trade loans +31% YoY. The August guidance cut to "low single digit" fee growth looks conservative against this run-rate.
- *Margin:* NIM 1.68% (-6bp QoQ, -14bp YoY); 9M avg 1.75%; **FY26 NIM guidance cut to below 1.75–1.80%**. NII S$2.28bn (-1% QoQ). Funding costs remain the drag; this is a headwind, not a catalyst.
- *FY27 guidance:* loans mid-single-digit, NII **above** 2026, fees mid-to-high single digit, costs mid-to-high single digit, credit costs 25–30bp. This is the positive: an NII-growth year after two of decline.
- *Loans* S$372bn (+6% YoY, +3% QoQ).
- *Other income* S$370m (-41% QoQ) — volatile line, mean-reversion possible.

**Capital generation.** CET1 **14.6%** (15.4% in 2Q26) after the interim dividend; leverage ratio 6.3% (6.8%). LCR 152%, NSFR 112%. The S$2bn buyback (2025–27) was ~40% done by 2Q26; management targets CET1 "slightly above 14%" post-programme, so the cushion is thinner than DBS/OCBC. UOBAM sale to Allianz GI (S$555m, ~+14bp CET1, 2027 close) is a modest add. Self-funding: yes; headroom: shrinking.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Value-trap check — this is where UOB differs.** Group NPL 1.5% (from 1.6%) but **NPA coverage is only 86%** (269% with collateral) and **Greater China NPL rose to 5.0%** (from 4.8% in 2Q26; 3.1%/3.5% earlier in 2026) with Greater China coverage down to 40% (78% a year earlier). Total credit costs 24bp (guided 25–30bp); 9M allowances fell 65% — flattered by the 2025 pre-emptive build. Total allowance rose to S$258m QoQ (S$211m). Earnings weakness is mostly cyclical (NIM), but the Greater China real-estate credit trend is a genuine structural-risk watch item; it is the main reason this is not a clean "temporary" story.

**Hidden assets.** No land bank/cash pile above market cap (≈ S$64bn). Identified: UOBAM disposal gain (~S$330m pre-tax), excess capital above 14% CET1 (limited), and the ASEAN-4 ex-Citi franchise (8.5m customers) whose monetisation is not yet in margins. Property revaluation surplus: data not found.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD (negative bias; not a Value Trap, not a Gem).** UOB offers the lowest valuation (1.33x P/B, 11.6x P/E) and a 4.1–4.6% yield, and FY27 guidance (NII up, fees +MSD–HSD) points to a 2027 earnings recovery. But 3Q26 missed, ROE slipped back to 10.1%, NIM guidance was cut, Greater China NPLs keep rising with coverage at 40%, CET1 headroom is thinnest of the three, and the analyst community is cutting. Risk/reward is 0.9–2.6 : 1 — below 3:1. Hold existing positions; the entry to consider is S$35–36 (cushion over the S$33.25 low) or a volume-backed reclaim of S$40.5.
