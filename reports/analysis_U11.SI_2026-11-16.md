**Trend:** the inflection failed. 3Q26 net profit was S$1.25bn (-15% QoQ), about 9% below the S$1.38bn consensus. Management's FY26 ROE target of 12–13% is out of reach. 9M profit of S$4.17bn (+27% YoY) is up only because 9M25 carried the 2025 provisioning. (ROIC is n/m for banks, so ROE is used.)

**Catalysts, next two quarters:**
- *Fees (positive):* record S$733m (+19% YoY). Wealth +13% (9M), cards +25%, trade loans +31%.
- *NIM (turning less negative):* 1.68% in 3Q (-6bp QoQ). FY26 guidance cut to below 1.75–1.80%. With SORA at a 16-month high and a Fed hike priced, the case that NIM is close to its low (Maybank's view) has more support than a week ago. It still needs 4Q26 evidence (results due Feb 2027).
- *FY27 guidance:* NII **above** 2026, loans mid-single-digit, fees mid-to-high single-digit, credit costs 25–30bp.
- *Dividend:* FY26 final dividend expected to go ex around Apr 2027 (Apr 2026 pattern), inside the 6-month window.

**Capital generation (in place of FCF yield):** CET1 **14.6%** (15.4% in 2Q) after the interim dividend. Leverage ratio 6.3%, LCR 152%, NSFR 112%. The S$2bn buyback (2025–27) was about 40% done by 2Q26, and no November 2026 buyback notices were found. At roughly 11% ROE and a ~50% payout, UOB retains about 5.5% of equity a year, which funds MSD loan growth plus dividends. The capital return yield is about 4.7% in dividends plus about 1% in buybacks. The UOBAM sale adds about 14bp of CET1 in 2027. Headroom over management's ~14% post-buyback CET1 target is about 60bp, the thinnest of the three banks.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Value-trap check.** NIM compression is **cyclical**, driven by funding costs. Rising SORA and Fed-hike pricing now point the other way, but deposit competition (system LDR 69.8%) limits the benefit. Asset quality is the **structural watch item:** group NPL 1.5%, NPA coverage only **86%**, **Greater China NPL 5.0%** (3.1% at the start of 2026) with coverage 40%. Thailand, UOB's largest ASEAN unit: the BOT warns that NPLs will stay elevated through 2026, and household debt is 86.7% of GDP (4Q25). On the other side, UOB economists estimate Thai 3Q GDP at +3.0% YoY. 3Q credit costs of 24bp are flattered by the 2025 general-allowance buffer. Verdict: **not a value trap.** Earnings are cyclically depressed and the book is provisioned, but the asset-quality risk is not trivial.

**Hidden assets** (none exceeds the ~S$62.3bn market cap):
- UOBAM disposal gain of about S$330m pre-tax, about +14bp CET1.
- About 60bp of CET1 above the 14% target, roughly S$1.7bn on ~S$280bn RWA (derived).
- The ex-Citi ASEAN-4 franchise (8.5m customers), whose value does not yet show in margins.
- 2025 general-allowance buffer (pre-emptive build) that can be written back.
- Property revaluation surplus: data not found.

**Stress tests:**
1. **Rates reverse (cuts instead of the priced hike):** NIM falls to 1.60% → EPS -6–8% → about **S$35**. The upside version: +10bp NIM on ~S$540bn of interest-earning assets (derived from 3Q NII/NIM) ≈ +S$0.5bn pre-tax, ≈ **+8% EPS**.
2. **Regional credit shock (Greater China CRE plus Thai households):** credit costs at 40bp vs 25–30bp guidance ≈ -S$0.45bn pre-tax. A further Greater China NPL rise is covered at only 40%. Combined effect: EPS **-10–12%**, CET1 -30–40bp to about 14.2%, and a likely buyback pause. Price about **S$33–34**.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD.** Not a Gem and not a Value Trap.

Several things are better than a week ago:
- The stock is oversold (RSI 29.9) after an 11% drop over 6 sessions.
- CLSA and Maybank upgraded after the results.
- October institutional flows were positive.
- SORA and Fed pricing now favour NIM.

But the fundamentals have not changed:
- ROE is 10.1%, the lowest of the three banks.
- Greater China NPLs are 5.0% with only 40% coverage.
- CET1 headroom is the thinnest of the three.
- At P/B 1.30x (+1 SD vs the 1.15x mean), the price is fair, not cheap.
- Price is below every moving average, and 20-day OBV is still deeply negative.

At S$37.68 the base-case R:R is 2.2:1, below the 3:1 hurdle. Hold existing positions.

Two conditions would make this a **Speculative Buy**:
- a pullback to **S$36.5–36.8** with a stop at S$35.70 (4.3:1), or
- a volume-backed close above **S$38.20**, then above S$40.5.

A close below S$35.10 (the 2026 low) would invalidate the base.
