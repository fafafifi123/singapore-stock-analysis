## 1. Quality & Growth Filter

### ROE and ROIC (ROA proxy) trend — FY2023 to 2Q2026

Banks do not have a conventional "invested capital" base the way industrials do, so — consistent with sell-side convention — we track **ROE** (the primary profitability KPI UOB itself reports) alongside **ROA** as the closest analogue to ROIC.

| Period | ROE | ROA (proxy for ROIC) | NIM | Commentary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | 14.2% (core, ex Citi one-off integration costs) | ~1.1% | 2.09% | Record core net profit S$6.06bn; peak of the post-Citi-deal cycle ([Yahoo/Edge Singapore](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/uob-core-net-profit-fy2023-153249478.html)) |
| FY2024 | 13.3% (as reported) | ~1.1%–1.14% | 2.03% | Record net profit S$6.045bn, +6% y/y ([OCBC, 7 May 2026](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf)) |
| **FY2025** | **9.6%** (as reported) | ~0.85% | 1.89% | **Inflection (trough):** net profit fell 23% to S$4.68bn after a S$1,361m 3Q25 allowance charge (~S$0.6bn pre-emptive general allowance) ([OCBC](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf); [Singaporean Investor](https://www.thesingaporeaninvestor.sg/2026/03/20/united-overseas-bank-limited-summary-of-annual-report-for-fy2025/)) |
| 1Q2026 | 11.5% (annualised) | n/a | 1.82% | Allowances normalised to S$203m vs S$290m 1Q25 ([OCBC](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf)) |
| **2Q2026** | **11.8%** (annualised) | n/a | 1.74% | **Inflection (reacceleration):** net profit +10% y/y, +3% q/q to S$1.48bn; 1H26 net profit S$2.92bn, +3% y/y ([Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend)) |

**Read-through:** Not a straight-line decline — ROE troughed hard in FY2025 (~460bp below the FY2023 core peak) on one large pre-emptive allowance charge, then reaccelerated for two straight quarters. But the recovery has not closed the gap to management's 12–13% medium-term target, nor to peers: OCBC's peer table shows UOB's FY26E ROE (10.8%) trailing DBS (16.1%), HSBC (15.1%), Public Bank (12.2%) and Maybank (11.5%). Caveat: 2Q26 specific credit cost rose to 39bp from 29bp (1Q26), above the top of FY26 guidance (25–30bp) — the reacceleration needs 3Q26 confirmation ([Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-dividend-aug-26)).

### Revenue catalyst for 3Q/4Q 2026

1. **Wealth management** — AUM S$198bn (1Q26, +5% y/y), invested-AUM penetration 40%→42%, targeting a **doubling of wealth income by 2030 vs 2025**; 1H26 wealth fee income +16% y/y ([The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/uob-targets-fee-led-growth-as-citi-integration-yields-8-5-million-asean-customers); [OCBC](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf)).
2. **Citi ASEAN integration is now a cross-sell story** — systems/platform work "largely complete" per Deputy CEO Wee Ee Cheong, leaving 8.5m ASEAN customers to cross-sell into; card billings +7–12% y/y, trade-finance loans +19% y/y ([The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/uob-targets-fee-led-growth-as-citi-integration-yields-8-5-million-asean-customers)).
3. **NIM has a defined bottoming catalyst** — guidance 1.75–1.80% FY26, with SORA "bottoming out" and trending higher in 2H26 ([Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend)).

**Offsetting fact:** management just **cut** FY26 fee-income guidance from high-single-digit to low-single-digit (delayed fee deals, softer card income), and loan-growth guidance sits at low-single-digit ([Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-dividend-aug-26); [OCBC](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf)).

### Capital strength

- **CET1:** 13.4% (Q4 FY23) → 15.5% (FY24) → 15.1% (FY25) → 15.3% (1Q26) → **15.4%** (2Q26, post-dividend) ([OCBC](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf); [Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/uob-2q26-profit-dividend)).
- **MAS D-SIB minimum: 9.0% CET1** ([MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2015/mas-publishes-framework-for-domestic-systemically-important-banks-in-singapore)) → UOB runs **~640bp above** the floor.
- **Capital-return programme:** S$3bn package (S$0.8bn special dividend, 2025; S$2bn buyback 2025–2027), management committing to keep CET1 "slightly above 14%" post-payout ([UOB Group](https://www.uobgroup.com/uobgroup/newsroom/2025/3-billion-package-to-return-surplus-capital.page?path=data/uobgroup/2025/324)). Buyback **40% complete as at 31 Jul 2026** (22.3m shares, ~S$794m) ([Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-dividend-aug-26)).
- **Self-funded, no dilution** — comfortably funded by the capital buffer and ~50% payout ratio.
- **Bonus:** 5 Aug 2026 sale of UOB Asset Management to Allianz Global Investors (S$555m vs S$223m book = S$330m pre-tax gain) adds **+14bp CET1** on 2027 close, while UOB keeps distribution economics via an AGI partnership ([Allianz GI](https://www.allianzgi.com/en/press-centre/media/press-releases/20260805-agi-accelerates-growth-path-in-ap-with-agreement-to-acquire-uobam); [Dr Wealth](https://drwealth.com/uob-sold-its-funds-unit-for-s555-million-banking-on-the-customer-not-the-product/)).

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

### Value-trap check

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1Q2026 | 2Q2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NPL ratio | 1.5% | 1.5% | 1.5% | 1.6% |
| NPA coverage | n/a | ~88%¹ | 100% | 88% (306% incl. collateral) |
| NIM | 2.03% | 1.89% | 1.82% | 1.74% |
| Specific credit cost | n/a | n/a | 29bp | 39bp (vs 25–30bp FY26 guidance) |
| Loan growth (y/y) | 5% | n/a | n/a | 5% |

¹approximate. ([Singaporean Investor](https://www.thesingaporeaninvestor.sg/2026/03/20/united-overseas-bank-limited-summary-of-annual-report-for-fy2025/); [OCBC](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf); [Growbeansprout](https://growbeansprout.com/dbs-ocbc-uob-dividend-aug-26))

**Verdict:** NIM decline is textbook rate-cycle (common to all three SG banks, guided to stabilise 2H26). The NPL/credit-cost uptick is driven overwhelmingly by **one Greater China real estate account** (Greater China NPL 3.5%→4.8% in one quarter), against a S$354bn loan book where Greater China exposure is S$49bn (coverage ~1.9%) and US exposure S$15bn (coverage ~4.8%) ([OCBC](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf)). This reads as an **idiosyncratic, contained credit event on top of a cyclical NIM headwind** — not structural, book-wide deterioration — though the coverage decline and above-guidance credit cost, plus management's flagged SME/oil-price second-order risk, are legitimate watch items into 3Q26.

### Hidden assets screen

- **~640bp excess CET1** above the MAS minimum, with management committing to "slightly above 14%" even post-payout.
- **UOBAM/Allianz divestment** — monetises a sub-scale asset-management unit at 2.5x book, locking in S$330m gain + CET1 uplift while preserving fee economics via distribution partnership.
- **Citi ASEAN franchise** with integration cost now largely sunk — 8.5m customers, better marginal margins ahead.
- **Aa1/AA- credit ratings** — structurally lower funding cost vs regional peers ([OCBC](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/equity-research/2026_05_07-uob_sp.pdf)).
- **Data not found:** no additional off-balance-sheet marks or undisclosed asset value could be verified.

## 5. Execution & Stress Test

- **Entry:** S$41.14. **Stop:** S$40.00 (risk S$1.14, −2.8%). **Base target:** S$44.00 (reward S$2.86, +6.9%) → **R:R ≈ 2.5:1**. **Bull target:** S$47.50 (reward S$6.36, +15.5%) → **R:R ≈ 5.6:1**.
- **Verdict vs 3:1 bar:** Base case does **not** clear 3:1; only the Bull case does, and it requires 3Q26 credit-cost normalisation plus a fee-income beat against a just-lowered bar.

**Scenario stress tests:**
1. **Renewed rate cuts:** NIM undershoots to 1.65–1.70%, ~S$150–250m annualised NII hit, EPS −3–5% → retest S$38–39.
2. **Greater China credit contagion:** NPL toward 2.0–2.2%, credit costs 45–50bp, net profit −10–15% → retest S$35–36, tail to S$32.50.
3. **UOBAM/AGI deal delay:** forfeits +14bp CET1 and S$330m gain (2027 close, 8-market regulatory approval pending) — ~S$0.20–0.30/share NAV impact, not an earnings shock.
4. **Broad market/geopolitical selloff:** DBS+OCBC+UOB >50% of STI weight; a repeat of Feb 2026's US–Iran shock could drag UOB toward S$36–37 on multiple compression alone — liquidity-driven, not solvency-driven given the CET1 buffer.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD.** UOB is not a value trap — NIM compression is cyclical and industry-wide, the NPL uptick is concentrated in one identifiable Greater China account, CET1 sits ~640bp above the MAS minimum, the buyback is 40% executed alongside a self-funded, non-dilutive capital-return programme, and the UOBAM/AGI deal is a fresh capital-light value unlock. ROE has troughed and reaccelerated for two straight quarters, and an independent justified-P/B cross-check converges tightly with observable broker targets (Bear ≈ JPMorgan's S$32.50; Base ≈ consensus ~S$42.9–43.5). But it is not yet a high-conviction Gem: the stock trades within ~4–6% of consensus fair value, management just cut FY26 fee guidance and is running credit costs above its own guided range, and the base-case risk/reward (entry S$41.14, stop S$40.00, target S$44.00) computes to **≈2.5:1 — short of the 3:1 threshold**. Only the Bull case (S$47.50) clears 3:1, contingent on unconfirmed 3Q26 catalysts. Sentiment itself is split (RHB Buy vs CGSI/DBS Hold vs JPMorgan Underweight), reinforcing a HOLD rather than a directional call — this is a name to watch into 3Q26 for credit-cost and fee-income confirmation, not to chase at the current entry.
