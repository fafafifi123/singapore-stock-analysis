**ROE trend:** the FY25 dip has fully reversed, with 9M26 ROE back above the FY23–24 level. Profit from **Banking Operations reached a record S$1.90bn (+16% YoY)**, so the core bank is accelerating. GEH's mark-to-market bond losses are the drag on the headline number. GEH's 9M contribution was still +5% to S$941m, and new-business embedded value rose 14% in 3Q.

**Catalysts, next two quarters:**
- **NIM:** NII hit a six-quarter high of S$2.31bn. The quarterly NIM decline is narrowing (10bp → 6bp → 4bp). Management says higher US rates should lift NII "in coming quarters as assets reprice".
- **FY26 guidance raised twice:** loans growing low double digits, income at least mid-single-digit growth, NII flat to slightly down, and credit costs cut to 15–20bp.
- **4Q26 results with the final dividend (Feb 2027):** this is the next hard catalyst. The capital-return plan has about S$0.8bn of S$2.5bn left (per our 2 Nov report; 3Q status not restated by OCBC), which could come as a special dividend or buyback.

**Capital generation (used in place of FCF yield):** ROE of 13.5% with a 50% payout gives about 6.7% organic book-value growth. Fully phased CET1 held at 14.0% while loans grew 14% YoY, so internal capital is funding growth and dividends without dilution. The earnings yield is 6.4% (1/15.6x) and the dividend yield is ~3.5% on S$0.99 consensus DPS. Excess CET1 in S$ is **data not found** (RWA not retrieved). Cost-to-income was 39.2% for 9M.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Value-trap check: the weakness is cyclical, not structural.** NPLs fell to **0.8%**, the lowest of the three local banks, with 168% coverage and 6bp of credit costs in 3Q. Loans are growing double digits. NIM compression (-18bp YoY) reflects the rate cycle and is bottoming. Non-interest income is about 42% of income (record customer-flow trading of S$480m, wealth AUM of S$358bn). Inventory and asset-turnover tests do not apply to a bank.

**Hidden assets:**
- **Great Eastern (~93.7% economic interest):** embedded value was S$20.1bn at FY25 (Aug report), so OCBC's share is ≈ S$18.8bn, about **S$4.2/share or ~15% of the ~S$128bn market cap** (derived). The 2025 delisting failed, so GEH stays listed and OCBC remains in control.
- **Bank of Ningbo (20%):** the partnership was extended for 10 years in Sep 2026. BoN's 1H26 net profit was RMB16.6bn (+12%), so OCBC's equity share is roughly RMB3.3bn per half-year (derived). The stake's market value is **data not found**.
- **Excess CET1:** 14.0% fully phased, versus a MAS minimum of 9% including the conservation buffer. No cash pile exceeds market cap.

*Band method:* the P/B band uses OCBC's year-end price/NAV for FY2021–25 (1.01, 1.10, 1.07, 1.13, 1.27; AR2025): mean 1.12x, SD 0.10x. That is only five observations, so treat it as indicative. On this basis the stock is about **+10 SD** above its 5-year mean, a full regime change in valuation. Other sources put the long-run average at 1.13–1.22x. **EV/EBITDA is not meaningful for a bank:** deposits and borrowings are its raw material, not financing, so "EV" and "EBITDA" have no economic meaning here. P/B against ROE is the right lens.

**Two-stage DDM / justified P/B (BVPS S$13.73, COE 8.5%):** Stage 1 assumes 3 years at 13.5% ROE and a 50% payout, giving BVPS S$16.70 and S$2.52 of PV dividends. Stage 2 is an exit multiple on that BVPS:
- *Conservative* exit at 1.5x → **S$22.1**. Cross-check with single-stage justified P/B (ROE 12%, g 3%, COE 9.5%): 1.38x → S$19.0.
- *Base* exit at 1.9x → **S$27.4**. Cross-check (ROE 13.5%, g 3.5%, COE 8.5%): 2.0x → S$27.5.
- *Aggressive* exit at 2.3x → **S$32.6**. Cross-check (ROE 14.5%, g 4%, COE 8%): 2.6x → S$36.0.
- **Fair-value range: S$22–33** (S$19–36 at the extremes). The price is about 4% above the base case.

**6-month target: S$29.5 (unchanged).** Base S$29.5, bull S$32.5 (retest of the 52w high on the 4Q print and capital return), bear S$25.5. Including the final dividend, the expected total return is about +5–6%. **Sentiment:** OCBC was the only one of the three banks to beat in 3Q, while DBS and UOB missed on trading income. A rotation into OCBC as the steadier name is visible in last week's relative strength.

The hard stop at **S$26.90** sits about 0.5 ATR below the S$27.22 low from 4 Nov; a close below it would invalidate the base. Buying at today's price **fails the 3:1 test** in both cases. Only a pullback entry at S$27.2–27.5 meets it.

**Stress tests:**
1. **Rate cuts instead of the hikes now priced (–20bp NIM):** this costs about S$1.1bn of NII pre-tax (~-11% EPS), partly offset by gains on GEH's bonds. ROE would fall to ~12% and the justified P/B to ~1.7x, giving **~S$23–24 (-17%)**.
2. **Regional credit shock (China/HK property, Indonesia, tariffs):** credit costs rising from 6bp to 40bp means about S$1.3bn of extra provisions (~-15% EPS). The 0.8% NPL ratio and 168% coverage are the best cushion of the three banks. Estimated value **~S$24 (-16%)**.
3. **Broad market sell-off:** the STI is already 10.5% off its high, has RSI 32 and has slipped below its SMA200. De-rating to 1.5x P/B gives **S$20.6 (-28%)**. Reverting to the +2 SD historical band (1.31x) gives S$18.0.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD (accumulate on a pullback to S$27.2–27.5 or on a breakout above S$29.70).** Fundamentals are the best of the three banks: a record banking-operations profit, two guidance upgrades, a 13.5% ROE inflection, NPLs at 0.8%, and CET1 holding at 14.0% fully phased despite 14% loan growth. The tape has also improved, with the shakeout absorbed, a demand day on 10 Nov and outperformance against the STI. Valuation is the problem. At 2.08x book (about +10 SD versus the 5-year band) and 15.6x earnings, the price is above the DDM base value of S$27.4, and buying at S$28.61 offers only 0.5:1 base-case R:R. It is not a Value Trap, because earnings and asset quality are improving, but it is not a Gem at this price either.
