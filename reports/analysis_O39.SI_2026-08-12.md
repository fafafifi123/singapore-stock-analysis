# Oversea-Chinese Banking Corporation (O39.SI) — Hidden Gem Analysis

*Analysis date: 2026-08-12*

---

## 1. Quality & Growth Filter

**ROE/ROIC trend (3–5yr) — genuine inflection after a 2025 stagnation year**

| Year | ROE | ROA |
|---|---|---|
| FY2021 | 9.54% | 0.95% |
| FY2022 | 10.56% | 1.03% |
| FY2023 | 13.27% | 1.26% |
| FY2024 | 13.37% | 1.28% |
| FY2025 | 12.20% (dip) | 1.16% |
| 1Q26 | 13.0% (annualized) | — |
| 2Q26 | **14.4%** (annualized, +2.1pp YoY) | — |
| H1FY26 | 13.7% (annualized, +1.1pp YoY) | — |

(stockanalysis.com ratios; OCBC 1Q26 and H1FY26 press releases)

FY2025 net profit fell 2% YoY to S$7.42bn (from a record S$7.59bn in FY2024) as NIM compressed — this is the "stagnation" phase. 2026 shows a clear re-acceleration: 1Q26 net profit S$1.97bn (+5% YoY), 2Q26 S$2.22bn (+22% YoY, the first quarter ever above S$2bn), H1FY26 S$4.19bn (+13% YoY, a record half). This is a genuine inflection, not just a low base effect — it's driven by non-interest income (wealth, fees) offsetting NIM pressure.

*Note on ROIC*: ROIC/traditional FCF are not standard analytical tools for banks — leverage and deposit-funding are the business model itself, not a cost of capital to be "returned." I substitute ROE (return on shareholders' equity) and ROA (return on total assets, a leverage-neutral profitability check) as the bank-appropriate quality proxies, and substitute CET1 ratio + payout capacity for FCF/capital-return capacity (below).

**Revenue catalyst for the next 2 quarters**
- **Wealth management / Bank of Singapore**: Banking wealth AUM hit a record S$350bn in Q2FY26 (+13% YoY); wealth income was S$3.29bn in H1FY26 (+27% YoY), now **41% of group income**. Net new money inflows of S$11bn in H1FY26. Management is targeting continued double-digit AUM growth. (OCBC Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, Investing.com)
- **NIM trajectory**: NIM fell to 1.76% in 1Q26 (-28bps YoY) and exit-NIM was 1.67% in June 2026. Management guides NIM to *stabilize* in H2 2026 as SORA "gradually strengthens toward year-end" — consistent with UOB's house view that SORA bottoms near 1% in H1 2026 before drifting back to ~1.3–1.4%. (Investing.com transcript; Maxthon/UOB SORA forecast)
- **Insurance (Great Eastern)**: GEH has historically contributed 12–20% of group net profit (avg ~15%); FY2025 GEH embedded value rose 11.5% to S$20.1bn. (growbeansprout.com; Great Eastern FY25 results)
- **Cost-to-income**: improved to 38.5% in H1FY26 vs 38.7–39.3% in early-2026 quarters; FY26 guided to "low-40s" as investment spend steps up. (Investing.com transcript; OCBC 1Q26 release)
- **Loan growth**: FY26 guidance raised to "high-single to low-double digit" (customer loans S$347bn, +9% YoY in 1Q26), though management cautions Q2's exceptional +5% QoQ (M&A-driven) won't repeat at that pace. (OCBC 1Q26 release; earnings call)

**Capital generation / self-funding capacity**
- Fully phased-in CET1 CAR: 15.2% (1Q26) → 14.0% (2Q26, management's steady-state target). Transitional-basis CET1 was 17.0% at 1Q26. MAS requires D-SIBs (DBS/OCBC/UOB) to hold CET1 ≥ ~10% (6.5% Basel minimum + 2.5% conservation buffer, plus a D-SIB surcharge), so OCBC is running **several hundred basis points of excess capital**. Management explicitly says it does **not anticipate any equity raising**. (OCBC 1Q26/2Q26 releases; MAS D-SIB framework release)
- Dividend payout target: 50% ordinary payout ratio; FY2025 total payout was ~60% of net profit (50% ordinary + 10% special) = S$4.45bn. Interim FY2026 dividend raised to 47 cents (+15% YoY from 41 cents). (OCBC FY2025 release; growbeansprout H1FY26 quick take)
- A dedicated **S$2.5bn capital return program (2024–2026)** — special dividends (10% of net profit in FY24/FY25) plus buybacks — has ~S$780–800m left to be completed by FY2026, via buyback or an estimated S$0.18/share special dividend alongside the final dividend. (Bloomberg Feb 2025; Investing.com Q2 2026 transcript)

This capital strength is the direct GARP substitute for "FCF self-funding capacity" — a well-capitalized bank funding both ~9–11% loan growth and a multi-year capital return program from organic earnings, with no dilution, is the banking-sector equivalent of a strong free-cash-flow compounder.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment

**Asset quality — stable, not deteriorating**
- NPL ratio: 1.0% (FY2023) → stable at **0.9% for 8 consecutive quarters** through 2Q26.
- NPA allowance coverage: 114% (FY2022) → 151% (FY2023) → 151% (FY2025) → **163%** (1Q26 and 2Q26, unchanged). (OCBC quarterly releases)
- Credit cost guidance: 20–25bps, trending toward the low end. (Investing.com transcript)
- Watch item: OCBC's Hong Kong office CRE exposure is ~2% of its loan book — the highest of the three Singapore banks (DBS 0.6%, UOB 1.3%) — though average LTV is <50% with annual revaluation, and Citi noted OCBC's "stable" NPL trend "eased some concerns" about SG-bank CRE exposure. (Dr Wealth / DBS equity research summary; Yahoo Finance/Citi note)

**Cyclical vs. structural**: The NIM pressure is clearly **cyclical**, tied to the rate-cutting cycle — SORA is forecast to bottom around H1 2026 near ~1% and stabilize/rebound modestly thereafter (UOB house view), and management guides NIM to stabilize in H2 2026. There is no structural deterioration evident in credit quality, funding mix (CASA ratio 50.2%), or loan growth (+9% YoY). This supports a "cyclical trough, not structural decline" read — but note the stock has already priced this in aggressively (see Section 3).

**Great Eastern stake — sum-of-the-parts**
- OCBC owns **93.7%** of Great Eastern Holdings (GEH, SGX: G07). (Yahoo Finance / theedgesingapore, April 2026)
- Timeline: May 2024 voluntary offer at S$25.60/share (S$1.4bn) for the then-11.6% minority stake → GEH free float fell below 10%, trading suspended July 2024 → June 2025 conditional delisting exit offer at S$30.15/share (S$0.9bn) for the remaining 6.28%, stated as final → **delisting did not succeed**; GEH resumed SGX trading Aug 21, 2025. Chairman Andrew Lee confirmed in April 2026 there are "no further chances" on privatisation for now — the special-situation catalyst here has effectively been **shelved**, not advanced. (OCBC media releases; Bloomberg June 2025; Yahoo Finance/theedgesingapore April 2026)
- Current GEH market data (Aug 2026): share price ~S$17.08, ~946.6m shares outstanding, market cap ~S$16.2bn. OCBC's 93.7% stake is worth **~S$15.2bn at market value**. (stockanalysis.com G07 page)
- GEH FY2025 embedded value: S$20.1bn (+11.5% YoY), or ~S$21.2/share on an EV basis — implying OCBC's stake is worth ~S$18.8bn on an embedded-value basis, modestly above the market-value figure. (Great Eastern FY25 results release; minichart.com AGM notes)
- Caveat: some 2024-era commentary cited a GEH "2024 embedded value of S$38.08/share" as the benchmark analysts used to call OCBC's 2024/2025 buyout offers cheap. This is roughly double the FY2025 embedded-value-per-share figure I could source directly (~S$21) — the discrepancy likely reflects "appraisal value" (embedded value + a multiple on value-of-new-business) vs. strict embedded value; I could not reconcile the two methodologies with confidence, so I flag this as **uncertain/data conflict** rather than presenting either as definitive.
- **Verdict on hidden SOTP value**: Because OCBC already consolidates 93.7% of GEH (it is fully on OCBC's balance sheet and income statement, not an off-balance-sheet minority stake), there is limited "hidden" arbitrage left to unlock via a spin-off/re-rating trade — unlike a scenario where a bank holds a large *unconsolidated* minority stake in a listed insurer. The stake's ~S$15–19bn value represents roughly **11–13% of OCBC's current S$140bn market cap**, which is a meaningful but not extraordinary embedded contributor; the "hidden gem" angle here has already been substantially surfaced by the market via GEH's own re-rating (GEH shares up sharply post-reopening) and OCBC's own multiple expansion.
- Excess capital: as above, ~400bps+ of CET1 buffer above the ~10% D-SIB regulatory floor represents several billion SGD of unencumbered capital, funding both the buyback/special-dividend program and continued loan growth without dilution.

This is the single most important — and least "hidden gem" — finding of this report: OCBC's fundamentals have genuinely improved (see Section 1), but the **market has already re-rated the stock far beyond its own 5-year historical valuation bands**. The 52-week range is S$16.19–S$31.56 — the stock has nearly **doubled** over the past year and sits at/near an all-time high.

**Simplified 2-stage DDM sanity check** (illustrative only, highly assumption-sensitive; base dividend ≈ S$1.10/share, approximating the FY2026 run-rate of 47¢ interim + an estimated final + remaining special dividend)

- *Aggressive case*: 7% dividend growth for 5 years, 3.5% terminal growth, 8.0% cost of equity → **fair value ≈ S$29.5/share**
- *Conservative case*: 2% dividend growth for 5 years, 2.0% terminal growth, 9.0% cost of equity → **fair value ≈ S$16.0/share**
- Current price (S$31.19) sits **above even the aggressive DDM case**, reinforcing the "priced for perfection" read from the multiples table above.

**Analyst price targets** (as of Aug 2026): UOB Kay Hian S$29.70 (Buy), DBS S$30 (Buy), RHB raised to S$29.80; TipRanks consensus average ~S$29.65–30.71 (range S$20–S$37.23, 9 Buy / 2 Sell). (TipRanks forecast page; theedgesingapore/RHB coverage found via search)

**6-month price target range** (blending analyst consensus, DDM sanity check, and technicals):

## 5. Execution & Stress Test

**Risk/reward ratio**: Using base-case 6-month target of S$33 vs. hard stop at the S$27.48 technical support:
- Upside: S$33.00 − S$31.19 = **S$1.81** (~5.8%)
- Downside to stop: S$31.19 − S$27.48 = **S$3.71** (~11.9%)
- Risk/reward ≈ **0.49 : 1 — does not clear the 3:1 threshold; in fact the setup is currently skewed against the buyer.** Even using the bull-case target (S$36) vs. the same stop, ratio is only ~1.3:1. The trade only reaches ~3:1 if one uses the deeper S$25 structural support as the stop and the S$37.23 high analyst target as the reward — an unrealistic pairing for a 6-month horizon.

**Scenario stress tests:**
1. **Rate-cut scenario (accelerated/deeper cuts)**: NIM already down 28bps YoY and management's H2 "stabilization" call depends on SORA *not* falling further. A faster/deeper cutting cycle than UOB's base case (SORA bottoming ~1% in H1'26) would push NIM below the 1.67% June exit level, directly hitting net interest income (still the majority of group revenue). Partial offset: wealth/fee income (41% of group income in H1'26) is less rate-sensitive and has been the primary earnings driver in 2026, cushioning — but not eliminating — the hit.
2. **Regional credit-quality shock (China/HK CRE)**: NPL ratio (0.9%) and coverage (163%) are currently very strong, but from a very low base — a shock could see NPL formation rise quickly given OCBC's above-peer HK office CRE exposure (~2% of loans vs. DBS 0.6%/UOB 1.3%). High coverage provides a buffer for earnings/capital, but a shock would likely trigger multiple compression on top of any credit-cost increase.
3. **Broad market selloff**: The valuation section shows P/B could plausibly mean-revert from ~2.2x toward its 5-year average of ~1.13x in a broad de-rating — a purely multiple-driven ~45–50% downside scenario, independent of any fundamental deterioration. Given how far current multiples sit above historical bands (P/E ~6.5σ, P/B ~6.5σ above 5-yr mean), OCBC currently carries meaningfully more multiple-compression risk than a name still trading within its historical range.

## 6. Final Verdict

**HOLD** (quality intact, but not a "hidden gem" entry point today).

OCBC's underlying business genuinely qualifies as a quality compounder with a real 2026 growth inflection: ROE has re-accelerated to 13.7–14.4% after a 2025 dip, wealth management (Bank of Singapore + GEH-linked flows) is now 41% of group income and growing double digits, asset quality is pristine (0.9% NPL, 163% coverage), and the bank is running several hundred basis points of excess CET1 capital that fully self-funds both loan growth and a S$2.5bn buyback/special-dividend program with zero dilution risk. However, this is precisely the problem for a GARP/hidden-gem thesis: the market has already found and re-rated this story aggressively — the stock has nearly doubled off its 52-week low, trades at a P/E (~17.9x) and P/B (~2.2x) roughly 6+ standard deviations above its own 5-year averages, sits above even the aggressive-case DDM fair value, shows an overbought RSI (72.7), and — tellingly — most sell-side price targets and the consensus average currently sit *below* the spot price. The Great Eastern "special situation" catalyst has also been explicitly shelved by management (no further privatisation attempt planned), removing a key optionality trigger. With risk/reward at the current price failing to clear even 1:1 on a 6-month view against the nearest technical support, this is a name to admire operationally and revisit on a pullback toward the S$26–28 zone, not to chase at all-time highs.
