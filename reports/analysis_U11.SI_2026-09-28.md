**Inflection read:** The FY2025 ROE collapse (14.2% core FY2023 → 9.6% reported FY2025) was driven almost entirely by a **S$615m pre-emptive general allowance taken in 3Q2025** in response to US/Greater China commercial real-estate (CRE) headwinds — not by a deterioration in core banking economics [finews.asia](https://www.finews.asia/finance/44309-uob-wee-ee-cheong-financial-results-3q25-pre-emptive-allowance), [The Edge Singapore](https://www.theedgesingapore.com/capital/results/uob-reports-lower-3qfy2025-earnings-after-making-pre-emptive-provisions). ### Capital-generation strength (bank analogue to FCF)

- **CET1 ratio: 15.4%** post-dividend as of 2Q26 (up from 15.3% in 1Q26; 15.1% at FY2025-end) [The Singaporean Investor 2Q26 review](https://www.thesingaporeaninvestor.sg/2026/08/07/united-overseas-bank-limited-sgx-u11-2q-1h-fy2026-results-review/), [The Singaporean Investor FY2025 summary](https://www.thesingaporeaninvestor.sg/2026/03/20/united-overseas-bank-limited-summary-of-annual-report-for-fy2025/). Against the **MAS D-SIB minimum of ~9.0%**, this implies a **~640bp surplus capital buffer** — one of the widest among SGX-listed D-SIBs.
- **Capital return programme:** UOB has committed to distributing **S$3bn of surplus capital (2023–2027)** comprising a 50-cent special dividend (paid 2025) plus **up to S$2bn in share buybacks**. Approximately **40% of the S$2bn buyback** had been completed by August 2026. Year-to-date to 11 Sep 2026, UOB was the **largest repurchaser among SGX banks**, spending **S$560.8m** to buy back 15.8m shares at an average price of S$35.39 [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/1106590213). Management's stated glide path is to bring CET1 down toward **~14%** via continued distribution — i.e., capital return capacity remains intact **without any need for dilutive equity issuance**.

## 2. Deep Value & Reversal Assessment — Value-Trap Check

**Is the weakness cyclical or structural?** The evidence points to **cyclical, single-name-concentrated** weakness rather than structural franchise deterioration — but the exposure has not yet fully healed and bears close monitoring into 3Q26.

- **Asset quality trend:** Group NPL ratio 1.5% (FY2024) → 1.5% (FY2025) → **1.6%** (2Q26, +0.1pp QoQ), with the deterioration "largely attributed to a single real estate account in Greater China" [The Singaporean Investor](https://www.thesingaporeaninvestor.sg/2026/08/07/united-overseas-bank-limited-sgx-u11-2q-1h-fy2026-results-review/).
- **Greater China concentration risk — the key red flag:** Total non-performing assets in Greater China rose from **S$1,330m (Jun-2025) to S$2,335m (Jun-2026)**, and new NPA formation for non-individual borrowers hit **S$902m in 2Q26** (vs S$341m in 1Q26). More critically, the **Greater China portfolio NPL ratio deteriorated sharply to 4.8% from 3.5% in just one quarter, while NPA coverage thinned from 57% to 42%** — flagged explicitly by sell-side (iFAST/FSMOne) as "the bank's largest residual credit risk" [Minichart / iFAST research note](https://www.minichart.com.sg/2026/08/24/uob-earnings-stabilise-but-broader-recovery-needed-target-raised-to-sgd-42-20/). Average loan-to-value on the office CRE book remains ~50%, providing a collateral buffer, and UOB's own commentary frames the exposure as "a few non-systemic corporate accounts" rather than a portfolio-wide problem.
- **Loan growth vs NIM:** Loan growth (+5% in 2Q26, ASEAN-4 trade loans +14% YoY) has been resilient even as NIM compressed to 1.74% (2Q26) from 1.78% (1H26 average, -18bp YoY) — consistent with a **rate-cycle-driven margin squeeze that is now inflecting positively** post the Sep-2026 Fed hike, rather than a loss of pricing power or franchise share.
- **Credit-cost guidance intact:** FY2026 total credit-cost guidance remains **25–30bp**, unchanged despite the Greater China flare-up — implying management does not (yet) view this as requiring a fresh round of pre-emptive allowances of the 3Q2025 magnitude.

### Hidden balance-sheet assets

- **Excess CET1 capital:** ~640bp above the D-SIB minimum (see Section 1) is effectively a "hidden" balance-sheet cushion supporting continued buybacks/dividends even if credit costs normalise higher.
- **Wealth/private-banking franchise value:** S$204bn HNW AUM (+7% YoY) [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/uob-asean-wealth-income-surges-30-as-high-net-worth-aum-reaches-s204-billion/) represents a recurring fee annuity not fully captured in a pure net-interest-income valuation lens.
- **Associate/property stakes, UOBAM sale:** AllianzGI has agreed to acquire UOB Asset Management, a transaction that — if completed — would crystallise value from a non-core asset-management stake onto the balance sheet [Allianz Global Investors press release](https://www.allianzgi.com/en/press-centre/media/press-releases/20260805-agi-accelerates-growth-path-in-ap-with-agreement-to-acquire-uobam). Detailed valuation/consideration for this transaction: **data not found**.
- Specific property revaluation surplus disclosures: **data not found** in sources accessed.

**Value-trap verdict for this section:** Not a value trap — the FY2025 profit collapse was a deliberate, well-flagged provisioning decision against a bounded, named exposure, and capital/liquidity metrics remain strong. But it is also **not yet a fully resolved situation**: Greater China coverage thinning to 42% means further specific allowances are a live risk into 3Q26/4Q26 reporting.

## 6. Final Verdict

# **Hold**

UOB is a genuinely improving, well-capitalised ASEAN franchise with real, name-able catalysts (Citi-integration revenue phase, 30% ASEAN-4 wealth-income growth, a NIM tailwind from the September 2026 Fed hike, and one of the widest CET1 buffers among SGX D-SIBs at 15.4% vs. a ~9.0% MAS minimum). ROE has clearly inflected off its FY2025 trough (9.6% reported) for two consecutive quarters (11.5% → 11.7%), and the balance sheet is actively returning capital via a S$2bn buyback (40% complete) without dilution risk (Section 1). Asset quality is not structurally broken — the FY2025 profit collapse was a deliberate, bounded provisioning decision, not a franchise failure (Section 2) — which rules out a **Value Trap** verdict.

However, it is **not currently a "Hidden Gem" at this specific price and moment**: the stock is trading at ~15x P/E, roughly **2.8 standard deviations above its own 5-year average**, and the market's ~S$43.30 consensus target sits almost exactly at the midpoint of a reasonably constructed S$37–S$50 two-stage DDM fair-value range — i.e., the market is already pricing in a fair amount of the recovery story (Section 3). Momentum has just delivered a clean technical breakout above S$42.33, but RSI at 74 is solidly overbought, and the resulting risk/reward **only clears the 3:1 threshold in the bull scenario**, failing it in the base case (Section 5).
